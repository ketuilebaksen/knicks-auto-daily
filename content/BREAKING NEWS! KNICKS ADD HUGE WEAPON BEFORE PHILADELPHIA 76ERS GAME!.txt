Five years, a lot of injuries, and one championship later, those two might be about to reunite at Madison Square Garden. And according to the latest reporting, Wiseman is now the favorite for the Knicks' final roster spot.

Start with Bobby Marks at ESPN. His read is that there's basically an open competition for the fourteenth roster spot between James Wiseman and Drew Eubanks — with John Konchar also in the mix. One of those three is probably making the roster.

According to The Stein Line, if Wiseman makes the Knicks' opening night roster, it will cement a reunion with Mike Brown, who was an assistant on Steve Kerr's staff during Wiseman's first two seasons with the Warriors. And the word, per Stein, is that Brown is indeed a Wiseman fan.

Stein also pointed out that Brown is now better known as the first championship-winning coach in New York since Red Holzman more than fifty years ago. Which means when Mike Brown likes a player, that opinion carries a lot of weight in this building right now.

And Stein confirmed the part we've been talking about all month. The Knicks are expected to fill only one of their two open roster spots — unless they make another move first that sheds salary.

So put it all together. One spot. A clear need for a third big behind Karl-Anthony Towns and Andre Drummond. And a head coach who has openly admired one of the candidates for five years.

Now let's talk about Wiseman honestly, because I like the story and I still have real questions.

Start with the Mike Brown quote. It's a great quote. But look at when it was said. 2021. About a twenty-year-old who hadn't had his major injuries yet. Since then, as Popper pointed out, Wiseman has had knee surgery and a torn Achilles, and he's played just a hundred and fifty-two games in six seasons.

So I wouldn't use that quote as proof of what Wiseman is today. It tells you what Brown saw in a healthy kid five years ago. The real question is how much of that player is still there.

And here's the irony that bothers me a little. What is a third-string center for? He's insurance. He exists for the nights when Towns is in foul trouble or Drummond is hurt. The entire job is being available when somebody else isn't.

And the biggest question about Wiseman is whether he can stay available.

That doesn't mean he's the wrong pick. It means the most important thing he can do in camp isn't a highlight dunk. It's showing up for every practice, every scrimmage, and every preseason game, healthy.

The second thing he has to show is the part of his game that has always held him back. Popper flagged his screen-setting and his defense as real red flags. And think about how Mike Brown's offense works. One big man with four players around him who are a little undersized for their positions. That big has to set hard screens for Jalen Brunson and be in the right spot on defense every single time. Rim-running and dunks are nice. Screens and positioning are the job.

So what's the case for him anyway? It's a strong one, and I understand why the Knicks are leaning this way.

Look at the free agent pool New York was working with. Out of everybody available, Wiseman is the best of a difficult situation. He's still young. He's six-eleven. He can rebound, block shots, and give you a real presence around the rim. Is he Mitchell Robinson? No. Nobody on the market was. But in terms of what was actually available, Wiseman fits the need.

And there's real value in the coach connection. Camp only lasts a few weeks. A player the head coach already knows — and already believes in — doesn't have to spend those weeks earning basic trust. He starts closer to the finish line.

And let's be fair to Drew Eubanks, because this isn't over. Eubanks is the safer option. He's a nine-year veteran who shot almost sixty percent from the field in Sacramento last season while playing through a thumb injury. He rolls hard, finishes, blocks shots, and knows exactly what his job is.

So the choice really comes down to this. Wiseman is the upside bet with a coach who believes in him. Eubanks is the reliable bet who's already proven he can handle this role. If Wiseman struggles or misses time in camp, that spot can swing back to Eubanks very quickly.

And what about John Konchar, the third name on Bobby Marks' list? He's not a center, so why is he even in this conversation?

Because the fourteenth spot isn't officially a "center spot." It's just a roster spot. Konchar is in the mix because he's the best version of the other option — using that spot on a wing who rebounds like a big and defends multiple positions. If Wiseman and Eubanks both have a rough camp, the Knicks could decide the smarter move is a versatile wing and simply live with small-ball lineups when Towns and Drummond sit.

I don't think that's where this ends up. But Konchar is the reason neither big man can coast. He's the reminder that if they don't earn it, the spot can go to a different position entirely.

Now here's why this decision matters more than most fourteenth-man decisions.

Every opponent knows how to attack this Knicks roster. Go at Towns. Get him into foul trouble. Then do the same to Drummond. Once both of them are in trouble, New York has to go small. And yes, they can play small. But that's not how this team is built. Mike Brown's system works best with one real big on the floor, and it gets uncomfortable fast when he has to throw it out mid-game.

So the third center isn't a minor detail. He's the answer to the one strategy every opponent will try.
