The title says the Knicks are signing a six-eleven monster center. They’re not. Nobody has signed anybody, and nobody has reported any talks. What the video actually covers is three real stories, and two of them are about why the Knicks just looked like champions again. First, the motion offense Mike Brown calls Nova, and the numbers that explain why the Bulls game felt so different. Second, Mohamed Diawara, who had his best game of the season. And third, a column arguing that the struggling Clippers could be a trade partner for a young, cheap backup center. That’s where the six-eleven part comes from. So let’s go through all three, honestly, and see what’s real and what’s just a headline.

Let’s start with the game itself. On Wednesday night at Madison Square Garden, the Knicks beat the Bulls one thirty-five to one-oh-five. That pulled them back to four and four after an oh and three start, with four wins in their last five games. Chicago had lost six straight. The Knicks erased an early six to four deficit with an eight to nothing run and never trailed again. They scored more than forty points in each of the first two quarters for the first time in franchise history, including forty-one to thirty-one in the second. They led eighty-four to fifty-two at halftime, and those eighty-four points tied the franchise record for a first half, a mark that goes back to November of nineteen eighty-eight. The biggest lead was forty-four. Jalen Brunson had thirty-three, with twenty-two in the first quarter alone. OG Anunoby added eighteen. Karl-Anthony Towns had seventeen points and twelve rebounds. Jordan Clarkson scored ten. And Dalton Knecht, on his two-way contract, scored ten in his Knicks debut. All of that without Josh Hart, who sat out with right ankle soreness.

But the most interesting part of that game wasn’t the score. It was how the Knicks got there.

Story number one: Nova. That’s the name Mike Brown uses for the Knicks’ motion offense. Some analysts call it a weave, because it’s built around a chain of handoffs. The idea is simple. It’s a four-out, one-in look. Two players sit in the slots, the spots just outside the three-point line above the elbows. Two more stand in the corners. And the center waits near the rim in the dunker spot. The first action is usually a handoff between the two slot players. In one example from the Bulls game, Tyler Kolek handed the ball to Mikal Bridges. If the defender doesn’t switch, Bridges attacks the paint right away. If that’s taken away, the next read is a handoff toward the corner. And the offense keeps flowing like that, read after read, until someone has an advantage.

The value of Nova was never about pretty baskets. It’s about reliability. When a set play breaks down, Nova gives the Knicks a plan to fall back on instead of standing around or forcing a bad shot. That’s why the early-season numbers in the video were so alarming. According to the analysis cited in the video, through the first eight games, the Knicks turned the ball over on about seventeen point two percent of their motion possessions. In set plays, the turnover rate was about thirteen point three percent. In freelance situations, when players just read and react, it was even lower, about eleven point four percent. In other words, the part of the offense that’s supposed to be the safest was producing the most mistakes.

And the Knicks went away from it. The video says that last season, motion made up somewhere between the high thirties and about forty percent of their half-court offense. This season, it had dropped to roughly twenty-one percent. And there’s one more warning sign. Last season, the Knicks had four games with a half-court turnover rate under ten percent. Through eight games this season, they hadn’t had a single one. I’d treat those exact percentages as the analyst’s numbers, but they line up with everything we’ve watched. Remember, in the Orlando loss, the Knicks had fourteen turnovers, eight in the fourth quarter. In Boston, they had six turnovers in the third quarter alone. After the loss in Philadelphia, Brown said the problems were defense in the paint and turnovers, and he said the team couldn’t try to hit home runs. It had to hit singles on offense. Nova is a singles offense.

Against Chicago, that changed. The video says the Knicks had their highest motion frequency of the season and their best efficiency running it. The ball moved, the spacing held, and the turnovers that had been killing possessions mostly disappeared. In the first half, the Knicks shot sixty-three percent and only had five turnovers. That’s what the system is supposed to do.

Now, let’s be fair. Chicago came in with one of the worst defenses in the league. Every team that had played the Bulls had scored at least one twenty-four points against them. So one blowout against a struggling defense doesn’t prove the offense is fixed. The Knicks have had real trouble scoring inside the arc this season, and they’ve been sloppy at times. They need to show they can run Nova this cleanly against good defenses. But the message from Wednesday is clear. When the Knicks trust the motion offense and run it with patience, they look like the team that won the title in June. And the biggest lesson is not to abandon it after a few bad possessions.

And there’s one more reason Nova matters so much for this team, and it’s Jalen Brunson. When the motion offense is working, Brunson doesn’t have to create everything himself. He can catch the ball on the move, coming off a handoff, already going downhill, instead of starting every possession from a standstill against a set defense. That saves his legs, and it makes him harder to guard. Coming into the Bulls game, Brunson was shooting just over thirty-nine percent from the field and about thirty-three percent from three, after offseason surgery on his left wrist. Against Chicago, with the ball moving, he went eight of ten in the first quarter. That’s not a coincidence. The better the system works, the easier the looks get for your best player.

Story number two: Mohamed Diawara. And honestly, this is the story I’m most excited about, because we’ve been talking about him for weeks. Remember, Brown said back in late October that the Knicks would hold the fort down with a veteran group of guys, and that he hoped to get to the point sooner than later where he could start throwing the young guys out there. Diawara only got his first real minutes of the season against Brooklyn on Monday, six minutes.

Against Chicago, with Hart out, he got a real chance. And he took it. According to the video, Diawara played a season-high twenty-one minutes. He finished with eight points on two of five shooting, one of four from three, and three of four from the line. But the points were the least important part. He also had six rebounds, four assists and two blocks. That stat line is exactly what made him interesting as a rookie. He doesn’t need to score a lot to help. He moves the ball, crashes the glass and protects the rim from the wing.

Let’s remember who Diawara is. He was the fifty-first pick in the twenty twenty-five draft. He played sixty-nine games as a rookie, which is a lot for a second-round pick on a contending team. And the Knicks signed him to a four-year deal worth about eleven million dollars, with roughly half of it guaranteed. They believe in him. He’s long, he’s versatile, and he’s willing to do the dirty work.

The biggest difference against Chicago was his approach. Too often this season, Diawara has played with hesitation, or he’s swung the other way and tried to do too much. When he hesitates, defenders sag off him and the offense stalls. When he forces it, he makes mistakes that send him to the bench. Against the Bulls, he found the middle. He played with confidence, attacked closeouts, made quick decisions, and stayed active on defense the whole time he was on the floor.

Now, let’s be realistic. Diawara is not about to become a thirty-minute player on a team this deep. And a lot of his minutes came after the Knicks had built a huge lead, so the pressure was low. But the Knicks need what he gives them. Without Hart, they’re thin on size at the wing. Landry Shamet and Jordan Clarkson have both played well off the bench, but neither brings much size. Diawara can guard multiple positions, bring energy and connect the offense. If his outside shot comes around, he becomes even more valuable. And with Dalton Knecht now in the mix as a two-way shooter, Diawara is still the one with the size and defensive tools the group is missing.

So what should Brown do? Keep testing him. Especially while Hart’s ankle is still a question. Saturday’s game in Cleveland would be a great test, because the Cavaliers bring a lot of size and physicality, exactly the kind of matchup where a long, active wing matters. A few more nights like Wednesday could solve a problem before it becomes serious.

Story number three: the six-eleven center. And this is where the title comes from. The video cited a column arguing that the Los Angeles Clippers could be the perfect trade partner for the Knicks’ frontcourt problem. Let me be clear right away: this is a columnist’s idea. There’s no report of talks between the Knicks and the Clippers.

Here’s the background. The video says the Clippers entered Friday’s games at one and seven. That’s a shock, because this was a team that traded Kawhi Leonard and got two All-Stars back, Darius Garland and Brandon Ingram, and then added Keaton Wagler with the fifth pick in the draft. On paper, they looked ready to compete. Instead, they’ve been one of the worst teams in the league. And their draft situation is a mess. The video says that because of the Leonard trade, they gave up five straight first-round picks from twenty twenty-nine through twenty thirty-three, and that they also owe a pick swap to Oklahoma City and a twenty twenty-eight first-round pick to Philadelphia. I’d treat those exact pick details as the video’s version, but the big picture is clear. The Clippers can’t count on the draft to rebuild. And a team in that position might be willing to move players for young pieces and future assets.

The column says the Knicks can’t afford the Clippers’ more expensive big men, like Brook Lopez or Isaiah Jackson, and that’s true. The Knicks are only about eight hundred twenty-four thousand dollars under the second apron, and above the first apron, they can’t take back more salary than they send out. So the column points to two cheaper, younger bigs.

The first is Yanic Konan Niederhäuser. He was the thirtieth pick, the last pick of the first round, in twenty twenty-five. He’s six-eleven and twenty-three years old. His game is simple: dunk, rebound, block shots, play with energy. The video says thirty-five percent of his made baskets last season were dunks, twenty-nine of his sixty-four field goals coming above the rim. And the most important part for the Knicks: he’s on a rookie contract that pays under three million dollars this season, with team options for the next two years. That’s exactly the kind of cheap, controllable deal the Knicks need.

The second is Baba Miller. He’s twenty-two, also six-eleven, and was the thirty-sixth pick in twenty twenty-five. He played college ball at Florida State, Florida Atlantic and Cincinnati. The video says four of his first six NBA baskets this season were dunks. His shooting is a question mark. In his best college season from three, he made thirty-nine threes at about thirty-four percent. In his other three college seasons combined, he made forty-one at about twenty-five percent. But his size and athleticism are real.

So let’s do the math the column didn’t do. Could the Knicks actually trade for one of these players? Yes, and it’s a lot simpler than the big-name trades we’ve covered. Pacôme Dadiet makes about two point nine eight million, and since the Knicks declined his option in October, he’s a pure expiring contract. If a player like Niederhäuser makes less than that, a straight one-for-one works right now, because the Knicks would be sending out more salary than they take back. Tyler Kolek, at about two point three million, could match a smaller contract too, if the numbers line up. And after December fifteenth, Diawara, Alvarado, Clarkson and Drummond all become trade-eligible, which gives the Knicks even more small salaries to work with. One-for-one deals like this also don’t touch the roster count, which matters on a team carrying fourteen players with almost no room under the second apron.

The bigger question is what the Clippers would want. A rebuilding team would want young players and picks. The Knicks only have one tradable first-round pick, in twenty thirty-three, because they owe their twenty twenty-seven, twenty twenty-nine and twenty thirty-one firsts to Brooklyn. But they have a lot of second-round picks. And for a player taken thirtieth or thirty-sixth, a second-round pick or two plus a young player like Dadiet could be a realistic package. That’s actually the most believable trade idea we’ve seen in weeks. No fake quotes, no impossible salary math, no superstar who doesn’t fit under the apron. Not a superstar. A cheap, young, athletic big.

Now, why do the Knicks need this at all? Because the backup center spot is still the biggest hole on the roster. Towns is the starter, and he’s been great on the glass. But there are fair questions about keeping him healthy and out of foul trouble for the rest of the season. Behind him, Andre Drummond is on a one-year veteran minimum deal. He’s still a great rebounder, but his finishing at the rim has been a problem, and he’s not a shot blocker. The video pointed out that Drummond set career highs last season with thirty-two made threes on ninety attempts, about thirty-six percent, in Philadelphia. With the Knicks, he’s barely shot from three at all. And James Wiseman, who won the last roster spot in training camp as the third center, has mostly played in garbage time.

And look at the teams the Knicks will probably have to beat in the playoffs. San Antonio, Oklahoma City, Cleveland, Boston. The video argues all of them look better or deeper at center than New York. Boston has Mitchell Robinson now, after he left the Knicks on a three-year, forty-seven-million-dollar deal. Cleveland has Evan Mobley. San Antonio has Victor Wembanyama. Oklahoma City has Chet Holmgren and Isaiah Hartenstein. To win another title, the Knicks will probably have to get through at least one of those frontcourts.

To be clear, neither Niederhäuser nor Miller is going to be Evan Mobley or Chet Holmgren. That’s not the point. The point is that either one could fill the most worrying hole on the roster for fifteen minutes a night, protect the rim, catch lobs, and do it on a contract the Knicks can actually afford.

And before the Knicks go shopping, they should give one more look to what they already have. James Wiseman is only twenty-five. He was the second pick in the twenty twenty draft. He’s had a brutal run of injuries, including a torn Achilles in October of twenty twenty-four, but in the preseason he had some strong moments, including eighteen points and ten rebounds in Toronto. Brown called him young, talented and raw. If Wiseman can give the Knicks ten solid minutes as a rim runner, the need for a trade gets a little smaller. If he can’t, then a cheap young big from a team like the Clippers becomes even more important.

There are a couple of caveats. Young bigs take time. A rookie or second-year center learning a new system in the middle of a title defense isn’t a sure thing. And the Clippers might not want to sell their young players at all. They might decide those are exactly the kind of players they need to keep for a rebuild. If the Clippers start winning, the whole idea goes away. But if they keep losing, the Knicks would be smart to make that call.

So let’s put it all together. The title said the Knicks are signing a six-eleven monster center. The truth is, there’s no signing. There’s a column suggesting the Knicks look at two young six-eleven bigs on the Clippers, and that idea actually makes a lot of sense, because the salaries fit and the need is real. But the bigger news from this week is the stuff that actually happened. The Knicks found their motion offense again against Chicago. And Mohamed Diawara showed he can be part of the solution on the wing.

A more honest title would be something like, the Knicks found their offense, and Mo Diawara found his game. Or, the cheap six-eleven trade that actually makes sense for the Knicks.
