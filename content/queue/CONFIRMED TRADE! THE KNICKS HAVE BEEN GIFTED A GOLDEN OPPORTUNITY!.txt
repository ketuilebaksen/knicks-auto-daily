So the front office is still looking for a third center. And as most of you have probably heard by now, Nick Richards is officially off the board. He signed with the Miami Heat. Widely expected, and now it's done.

But here's the thing. If the Knicks wanted Nick Richards, they would have signed Nick Richards a long time ago. They didn't. Because what they actually want is someone younger. Someone on a rookie scale contract they can mold into a long-term center.

Because Andre Drummond is not a long-term answer. He'll be very effective this year, but he's on a one-year minimum deal, and his role naturally shrinks the longer he sticks around. This season, yes, he's the backup center. But that doesn't mean New York stops hunting — which could get a little uncomfortable when it comes to who actually holds that job.

And that's exactly what we've been seeing all summer.

The Knicks have talked about Yves Missi a lot. They saw him as a potential replacement for Mitchell Robinson back when Robinson was still here.

Go back to December of 2025, when they first discussed the Alvarado trade with New Orleans. What the Knicks actually wanted was Alvarado and Missi. They were trying to move Guerschon Yabusele and somebody else — that piece rotated between Tyler Kolek, Deuce McBride, some picks.

And the Pelicans stood firm. They like Missi. They see him as part of their future.

But here's where it gets interesting. New Orleans has a genuine logjam in the frontcourt. And that's exactly why one of these guys is getting moved.

If they're starting Missi, sure, he's tough to get. But they just used a first-round pick on Derik Queen, and Queen has been solid. I don't think they want him as a pure backup to Zion Williamson. And Zion, when healthy, is one of the most talented players in this league — the reincarnation of Blake Griffin in every sense.

Then look at the rest of it. The Pelicans accidentally — and I'm not kidding, they did not mean to do this — gave DeAndre Jordan a second guaranteed year. This is not a great front office. But the roster itself is not bad at all.

They brought in Bennedict Mathurin, which was a great signing. And now they're over the limit. Somebody has to go.

Jordan. Missi. Karlo Matkovic. Derik Queen going into year two. That is a lot of big men for one roster.

And Shams Charania said it out loud on X — quote, I'm told to expect a small corresponding move from the Pelicans to open up space under the luxury tax and create a roster spot for Bennedict Mathurin. Keep an eye on that September eighth date.

September eighth. That's the date circled on the calendar.

And I want to explain exactly why that deadline has teeth, because this is the part that turns a rumor into a real opportunity for us.

Cap expert Yossi Gozlan pointed out that the Pelicans plan to use the roughly fifteen million dollar non-taxpayer mid-level exception to sign Mathurin. And here's the rule that matters — the moment a team uses that exception, they are hard capped at the first apron. Not soft-capped. Hard capped. They physically cannot go a dollar over it for the rest of the season, no exceptions, no penalties you can just pay your way out of.

But Mathurin's projected contract puts them above that line. Which means they have to shed payroll first for it to work at all.

So this isn't New Orleans idly considering a trade. This is a team that has already decided who they want and now has to clear the runway to land him. That's a motivated seller with a clock running. And motivated sellers with clocks are where good front offices make their money.

The Knicks fit perfectly into that hole.

Now, Yahoo Sports made the point directly: if the Pelicans are willing to accept a few future second-round picks for their backup center, meaning Missi, he'd go a long way toward filling the void Mitchell Robinson left behind. Similar to Mitch, he won't make a big offensive impact outside of being a lob threat, and he's a poor free throw shooter. But he's a talented rebounder and a major difference-maker on defense.

And the numbers back that up. Missi played nineteen point six minutes a game last season. That is almost exactly what Mitchell Robinson played. He put up five point seven points and five point eight rebounds. No, those aren't Mitch's numbers, let's be honest. But he blocked one and a half shots per game and he's a good offensive rebounder.

Drummond is a decent defender, but he's a big drop off from Robinson. Missi is closer, and he could be coached up to deliver similar numbers. At minimum, he gives Mike Brown more options — including more chances to run Karl-Anthony Towns at the four.

And I love seeing lineups mixed around Towns. He even said himself he didn't really know his role going into last season, and then he figured it out. Now you can use him at power forward or center. It feels like Towns knows exactly what he's doing in a Mike Brown system — similar to how Brown used Domantas Sabonis.

But here's my honest read on Missi. He's tough to acquire, and I don't think you get him for second-round picks. New Orleans genuinely views him as a long-term starter. If Jeremiah Fears pans out, if Mathurin keeps performing, if Zion stays healthy, and if one of Missi, Matkovic, or Queen works out at center — that's a real team. I think the Pelicans are underrated.

And Missi is working out. That's the whole problem. That's exactly why the Knicks want him.

So let's talk about the other option. Because there's a guy who's a lot easier to acquire, and honestly? This is the move I would absolutely love. It gives you versatility, it gives you a shot blocker, and it gives you a solid rebounder. Not a great one, but a solid one.

That's Karlo Matkovic.

Matkovic played just under fifteen minutes a game last season and blocked a shot per game in that time. And he shot the ball excellently. His true shooting was sixty-nine point seven percent. Sixty percent from the field. Forty-two point two percent on low-volume threes — but he did take them. And he's a great lob threat.

Now, I'll be fair and give you the caution here, because I don't want to sell you a fantasy. Fifteen minutes a night is a small sample, and elite efficiency on low volume has a habit of regressing when a player's role expands. Nobody is guaranteeing sixty-nine percent true shooting if you double his minutes and defenses actually start game-planning for him.

But that's not what we'd be asking him to do.

Because the Knicks don't need Matkovic to be an elite rebounder either. Look at who's already here. Andre Drummond, one of the best rebounders of this generation. Karl-Anthony Towns, one of the best rebounders of this generation. Josh Hart, who might be the best rebounder under six-ten in the NBA — and he's six-four. And OG Anunoby has been a good rebounder too.

We have rebounding. I'm not worried about that with Matkovic. Yeah, he should get better at it and I hope he does. But that's not the job.

What Matkovic gives you is a player you can use in a few different roles. And because he's different from Missi, he doesn't demand the huge role Missi would instantly command. You can use Matkovic in spot minutes without it being a whole thing.

And he's gettable. Second-round picks. The Knicks already tried to trade for him once and it fell through. But now the Pelicans have to make a corresponding move and they've got a mountain of bigs on that roster. This is the opening.

Dig into the shooting a little more and it gets better. Matkovic shoots thirty-five point seven percent from above the break. He's excellent below the rim catching lobs. And he shoots sixty-one percent from the left corner on threes. That's an effective shooter on low volume — he's not pulling from deep non-stop, but when he takes it, it goes in.

And putting him next to Karl-Anthony Towns for stretches against bigger lineups? That's fun. Especially since we're evidently going to be seeing San Antonio a lot more. Who knows, maybe we get a Finals rematch and win back-to-back titles. Weird thing to even be able to say out loud.

But that's the real value. Matkovic lets you overload bigs against big teams. Think about Oklahoma City wanting to run Isaiah Hartenstein and Chet Holmgren together. If you made me bet on next year's Finals right now, I'd say Thunder and Knicks. And in a series like that, I am absolutely putting Matkovic on the floor next to Towns.

So let me be clear before the comments come. This is not an anti-Missi video. I would love Yves Missi. I'm just being realistic with you.

Can the Knicks acquire Missi? Yes. Will it cost a lot? Yes. Will it effectively be an overpay that isn't actually great for New York? Probably, yeah.

At the end of the day, here's what matters for this team. Get a guy who can block shots. Get a young guy on a cheap contract.

And look at the pattern, because once you see it you can't unsee it. Every single center this front office has looked at checks the same three boxes. Young. Cheap. Blocks shots. Moussa Cissé, who they signed to an offer sheet before the Mavs matched it. Moussa Diabaté, who they couldn't get. Ryan Kalkbrenner on the Hornets, who everybody seems to have forgotten about. And now Matkovic and Missi. Same profile every single time.

And that pattern tells you something important — it tells you what they've already ruled out. They are not chasing a veteran name to make the fan base feel better. They're not signing a thirty-four year old for one season of familiarity. They've decided the third center spot is a development slot, not a rental, and every name that leaks confirms it.

Matkovic is the cheapest of them. Which now makes him the most realistic. And the Knicks should be all over this before September eighth.
