Maxwell Ogden of Daily Knicks wrote that a McBride extension could still be negotiated — but if the Knicks can't secure his future, New York should call the Orlando Magic and see about swapping him for Tristan da Silva.

And Ogden argues it helps both teams take a real step forward.

For Orlando, it starts with need. They desperately want a guard who can space the floor, defend, and help Desmond Bane take pressure off of Paolo Banchero and Franz Wagner. McBride does all three of those things immediately.

And for us? Ogden says acquiring da Silva brings in a wing who offers a proven jump shot and respectable off-ball defense.

So let's actually look at the player.

Da Silva averaged ten points and one point six made threes in twenty-four point seven minutes a game last season. A solid campaign for a second-year forward. And those numbers scale up to fourteen point four points and two point three made threes per thirty-six minutes.

But the growth is the real story here. From year one to year two, he improved by two point six points and half a made three per thirty-six. And his slash line jumped from forty-one percent from the field, thirty-three from three, and eighty-seven at the free throw line — up to forty-five, thirty-seven, and eighty-eight.

And that free throw number matters more than people realize. It's one of the oldest scouting heuristics in basketball: if a young player is knocking down free throws at a high rate, the three-point shooting usually follows. It signals clean mechanics and repeatable touch. So when you see eighty-eight percent from the line paired with a jump to thirty-seven from deep, that's not a fluke season. That's a shooter arriving.

And this move solves something real for us.

Because outside of Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, and Josh Hart — all of whom are starters — we are thin on the wing. Yes, we have Mohamed Diawara. But Diawara is still pretty raw, and you cannot build a title defense on a hope.

And think about what that thinness actually means over eighty-two games. If any one of those three starters misses time — and across a full season, somebody always does — you are immediately asking a raw second-year player to hold a rotation spot on a championship defense. That's not a depth chart. That's a prayer.

So there's genuine logic in adding another young player who gives this roster a more secure future at a very cheap price.

The Knicks need to start saving. That's not a preference, it's an inevitability. And it already started with the decision not to re-sign Mitchell Robinson, because doing so would have pushed us into the second apron. That's done. That choice has been made.

Think about what that means. The Knicks would be replacing Deuce — who has become more and more of an off-ball player — with a different off-ball player who happens to be significantly taller. And height on the wing is something this roster genuinely needs.

Deuce McBride lost significant minutes in the NBA Finals. He averaged twenty-six minutes a game in the regular season. In the Finals, he was down around fourteen to sixteen. That's a drop of roughly ten to twelve minutes a night, in the biggest games of his life.
