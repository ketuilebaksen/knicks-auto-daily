## THE HONEST HEADLINE

Clarity first, the NBA Tunnel way. There is no trade, and there is no confirmed signing. The Lakers waived Lawson Lovering from his two-way contract earlier this week, and some Knicks analysis has suggested he could be a fit as a developmental backup center. That's it. The Knicks haven't reportedly made any move, and nobody has reported talks. We'll go through why the idea makes sense — and what it would actually cost.

## THE PACERS GAME: HOW IT SLIPPED AWAY

**The start:** The Knicks jumped out to an early 18–9 lead in the first quarter. Then Indiana took control and pushed the lead to 14.

**The comeback:** The Knicks kept fighting and tied it early in the fourth quarter.

**The run:** Then the Pacers ripped off a 10–0 run midway through the fourth.

**The ending:** The Knicks got one last chance when Mikal Bridges' pressure forced a turnover on an Indiana inbounds play with under three seconds left. KAT got the look. The turnaround fadeaway didn't fall.

**The missed chance:** Pascal Siakam missed two free throws with 23 seconds left that could have sealed it. The Knicks couldn't take advantage.

For a team that made a habit of winning these moments last season, it was an unfamiliar feeling.

## THE STARS DID THEIR PART

**Jalen Brunson:** 33 points and 11 assists, including 16 points in the third quarter alone. After Pacers guard Quenton Jackson got in his ear, Brunson smiled — and then went on a tear that pulled the Knicks right back into the game. He cooled off in the fourth and wasn't involved in the final play.

**Karl-Anthony Towns:** 23 points, 15 rebounds, and five assists — and franchise history. That was his 12th straight double-double to open the season, a new Knicks record, passing the legendary Willis Reed. On a night that ended in heartbreak, that's a moment worth celebrating. Twelve games, twelve double-doubles, on a team defending a championship — while his own contract is still unresolved. That's commitment.

**The Pacers:** Playing without Tyrese Haliburton, Indiana got balanced scoring. All five starters reached double figures: Pascal Siakam with 19, Aaron Nesmith with 18, Kelly Oubre Jr. with 16, and Ivica Zubac and Andrew Nembhard with 12 each.

**The even stuff:** Both teams made 39 of 90 shots. Indiana committed 15 turnovers to the Knicks' 13. So the game came down to the glass, the free-throw line, and a few key possessions late.

## TAKEAWAY #1: THE KNICKS KEEP PLAYING SMALLER THAN THEY ARE

This is the big one. This team prides itself on rebounding — they've been at or near the top of the league in rebounding percentage. Yet they lost the battle on the glass to a Pacers team that has been one of the worst rebounding clubs in the league.

- **Indiana's offensive rebounds:** 17
- **KAT's rebounds:** 15
- **The other four Knicks starters combined:** just 11
- **Zubac:** 13 rebounds for Indiana, with the rest of the Pacers' starters adding 19 more

That's a huge problem. KAT can't do it all alone, every single night.

And the free-throw line told the same story. In the first half, the Pacers shot 20 free throws to the Knicks' three. That came right after the Cleveland loss, when the Cavaliers took 25 free throws to New York's 11. Mike Brown has stressed playing with force, and in these two games, the Knicks haven't been the more physical team.

**The fix:** Josh Hart, who just returned after missing time with the ankle injury and then an illness, should help a lot. And Brown may need to try more minutes with Towns alongside Andre Drummond if the problem keeps showing up.

## TAKEAWAY #2: THE FRUSTRATION IS BOILING OVER

Late in the first half, OG Anunoby appeared to get fouled on a drive with no call. Mike Brown picked up a rare technical foul — and his team responded. Brunson and Tyler Kolek helped cut a 14-point deficit to single digits.

Early in the second half, with New York down 12, Brown called a quick timeout, said a few words, and let his players handle it. KAT and Bridges each hit a three, Brunson dropped in a floater, and Bridges added a jumper to get within five.

But in the fourth quarter, with the Knicks trailing by just two, Towns was hit with a technical after not getting a call he felt he deserved. Two minutes later, Indiana's lead was back up to 12.

Three technical fouls in a game decided by two points. That's the sign of a team that's still unsettled. The games the Knicks found ways to win last spring are the games they're finding ways to lose this fall.

And let's be fair: frustration with the officiating happens to every team. But champions channel it into the next possession, not the next technical. That's the lesson from Indiana. Brown's first-half technical seemed to wake the team up. KAT's fourth-quarter technical did the opposite. The difference was timing.

## TAKEAWAY #3: THE OFFENSE HASN'T LOOKED LIKE ITSELF

The Knicks have scored over 100 points in 11 of their 12 games. But look closer. In three games against Milwaukee and Chicago, they averaged 128 points. In the other nine? Around 104. That's not a big number for this group.

**The good news:** It looks more like a temporary slump than a broken system, given how strong this offense has been in recent seasons.

**The bad news:** The schedule is getting tougher, and the Knicks need to find their rhythm quickly. A loss in November doesn't feel urgent, but these games add up — especially in a deeper East. And with the Cup group record at 0–2, their hopes of defending the NBA Cup title are hanging by a thread.

## THE CENTER QUESTION — AGAIN

The battle on the boards in Indiana put the spotlight right back on the question that's followed the Knicks since opening night: what happens at center when Towns isn't on the floor?

**Andre Drummond:** He was brought in to handle the backup role. He hasn't been overly impressive overall, but he's rebounded better than many expected — and extra possessions matter for a struggling offense. His limit is foot speed, especially late in close games when opponents attack him in space. And if Towns ever missed time, the Knicks would be leaning on Drummond for far more minutes than they'd like.

**James Wiseman:** The third center has played just 26 minutes through 12 games. His playing time has been inconsistent, but when he's played, he's impacted the offensive glass. Per 36 minutes, Wiseman is grabbing more offensive rebounds than anyone on the roster — roughly 50% more than Drummond. Still, a third center who barely plays leaves the Knicks thin if injuries or foul trouble hit.

For a team with championship expectations, that's not ideal — especially after a night when Indiana grabbed 17 offensive rebounds. Which brings us to Lovering.

## THE LAWSON LOVERING SCOUTING REPORT

**The basics:**
- **Age:** 23
- **Height:** 7 feet
- **Wingspan:** 7-foot-5
- **College:** Utah, undrafted

**The Lakers chapter:** According to HoopsHype's Michael Scott, the Lakers waived him on November 10th, and no team put in a claim. Los Angeles had signed him to a two-year, two-way deal on October 28th, then moved on less than two weeks later to sign center Carson Cooper instead. Lovering appeared in just two games for the Lakers, playing about two minutes in total. The quick release says more about the Lakers' situation — they're off to a 10–2 start and didn't have the room to develop him — than about Lovering himself.

**The Memphis chapter:** He spent time with the Memphis Grizzlies, who signed him to a 10-day contract last February. In his NBA debut in Denver, he posted three points, four rebounds, and two steals in 18 minutes. With Memphis' G League affiliate, the Hustle, he appeared in 30 games and averaged 7.7 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 1.3 blocks in 26.8 minutes per game.

**The grind:** Memphis let him go in late September after a training camp deal. He spent a month as a free agent before the Lakers called. That kind of journey is very common for undrafted big men — and it often produces players who understand their role and are willing to do the dirty work winning teams need.

**The game:** He's a sturdy, no-frills big. He sets screens, crashes the glass, runs the floor, finishes at the rim, and protects the paint. He's not a shooter.

## WHY HE FITS THE KNICKS

**The job description is clear.** The Knicks mainly want their backup centers to rebound, defend, and leave the outside shooting to others. Drummond showed some three-point range last season, but he's attempted just one three this year. Lovering fits that job perfectly.

**The style.** Compared with Wiseman, Lovering offers a different kind of upside — less flash, more structure, and a defensive mindset that matches the way Mike Brown wants this team to play. For a coaching staff that values effort and physicality, that profile is easy to like.

**The age.** At 23, he's young enough to grow into a bigger role over the season.

**The cost.** This is the part that makes it realistic. Because he'd come in on a two-way contract, it wouldn't touch the main rotation or the salary picture — huge for a team with only about $89,000 of room under its hard spending line.

## THE ROSTER MATH

Here's the catch: the Knicks' two-way spots are full. Signing Lovering would most likely mean letting go of Jaden Akins from his two-way spot.

Would that be a big loss? Probably not. The Knicks have plenty of guards — Jalen Brunson, Deuce McBride, Tyler Kolek, Landry Shamet, Jordan Clarkson, and Jose Alvarado. Akins' role isn't essential right now. And Dalton Knecht, who also holds a two-way spot, has already shown he can contribute.

Swapping a surplus guard for a developmental big would address a clear need without touching the main rotation or the salary picture. It's the kind of low-risk move contenders make all the time during the season.

**My verdict:** ✅ It makes sense. Not as a savior — as insurance. Lovering isn't going to fix the rebounding problem by himself. But a 7-footer with a 7-foot-5 wingspan who rebounds, blocks shots, and accepts a role is exactly the type of cheap depth the Knicks need. If he's still available, it's worth a look.

And here's the smart part: it's not either-or. The Knicks could sign Lovering now as cheap insurance and still make a bigger frontcourt trade after December 15th. One doesn't block the other.

## DEUCE McBRIDE: "YOU NEED ME"

And now the bright spot from the last week. Deuce McBride is sending the Knicks a simple message: you need me.

**The season numbers:**
- 19.2 minutes per game
- 8.0 points per game
- 44% from three

**The turnaround:** After a rough night in Chicago, when he went one-for-seven from deep, McBride has averaged 9.1 points and hit 46% of his threes. He buried five threes against Cleveland and three more in Indiana. When the ball swings his way, he's ready.

**The defense:** His defensive rating is 109.6 this season, down from 116.1 last year. Small sample, but the sign is clear: he's competing at the point of attack, staying in front of ball handlers, and giving Mike Brown a guard he can trust. Remember, Brown called him the defensive player of the game in the Bucks blowout.

**The fit:** With Brunson carrying the scoring and Kolek handling the backup point guard duties, the Knicks need guards who can play off the ball, space the floor, and defend without needing plays called for them. Deuce does all three. He doesn't need the ball. He takes what the game gives him — and lately, the game has been giving him open threes.

**The energy:** He picks up ball handlers full court, fights through screens, and rarely takes a play off. On nights when the Knicks look flat, those effort plays change the tone.

## WHY THE KNICKS SHOULD KEEP DEUCE

His name has come up in trade talk all season — he's on an expiring deal, and we've discussed him as a fit for Oklahoma City and as a salary piece in the Trade Calculator. But the case for keeping him gets stronger every week.

**The window:** The Knicks are defending champions. Their core isn't getting younger. The window to win more titles with this group won't stay open forever.

**The two kinds of value:** On one side, the picks and future assets a Deuce trade might bring. On the other, what he gives them on the court right now. For a team built to win now, the second one matters a lot more.

**The message:** Trading a productive rotation player for future picks in the middle of a title defense would send the wrong signal. Keeping him says the Knicks are focused on the present.

**The caveat:** That doesn't make him untouchable. Things change over a long season. If the right deal shows up later, the front office could revisit it. But mid-November isn't the time.

Looking back, one of the smartest decisions the Knicks made heading into this season might be the move they didn't make — holding on to McBride. That's starting to look like a quiet win.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. The Knicks sign a developmental big to a two-way deal before December — whether it's Lovering or someone with the same profile — and they release Akins to make room. Deuce stays a Knick through the deadline and keeps shooting over 40% from three. With Hart back, the rebounding bounces back in Toronto, and the Knicks win both games there. KAT extends the double-double record to 14. And the technical fouls? Mike Brown gets them settled down — this group is too experienced to keep beating itself.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. There's no trade and no confirmed signing — the Lakers waived 23-year-old, 7-foot center Lawson Lovering from his two-way deal, and he's been floated as a fit for the Knicks' backup center need. He's a no-frills big with a 7-foot-5 wingspan who averaged 7.7 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 1.3 blocks in the G League, and signing him would likely mean cutting Jaden Akins from a two-way spot, with no impact on the salary picture. The Knicks lost 106–104 in Indiana to fall to 6–6 and 0–2 in Cup play, getting out-rebounded and out-shot at the line despite Brunson's 33 and 11 and KAT's 23 and 15 — his record-breaking 12th straight double-double to open the season. And Deuce McBride is shooting 44% from three with his best defense in years, making the case to keep him stronger every week.
