The Knicks have two open roster spots and they're sitting roughly three point two million dollars below the second apron.

Now, you'd look at that and think, three point two million, a veteran minimum is well under two million, so surely you fit two of them.

You can't. A minimum contract counts against the cap and the apron at its full value based on the player's years of service — not at the reduced number the league reimburses teams for. So a genuine veteran minimum eats a considerably bigger piece of that three point two million than the headline salary suggests.

Fill both spots, and you cross the second apron. And crossing it for the defending champions isn't a line item, it's a straitjacket. You lose the ability to aggregate salaries in a trade. You lose the taxpayer mid-level. Your future first-round picks start getting frozen. It's the hardest constraint in the modern collective bargaining agreement, and this front office has made very clear all summer that they will not touch it.
