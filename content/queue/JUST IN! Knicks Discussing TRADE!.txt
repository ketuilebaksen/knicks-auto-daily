The Knicks are trying to make a trade, and this time there's a deadline. Monday afternoon. If nothing happens by then, Bruce Brown is probably gone. That comes from Ian Begley of SNY, and it lines up with what Brett Siegel of ClutchPoints and Stefan Bondy of the Post have been reporting all week. And unlike most of the trade stories we've fact-checked on this channel, this one is real.

But "the Knicks are discussing a trade" sounds a lot bigger than what's actually on the table. This isn't a blockbuster. It's a small, technical deal to move one young player off the books so the Knicks can keep two veterans under the second apron. And there's a real chance the player who goes isn't the one most fans expect.

Welcome back to Ketuil KNICKS. Today: what the reporters are actually saying, the two players most likely to be moved and the case for each, the math, why this is harder than it sounds, and what it tells you about how this front office thinks.

Let's start with exactly what's been reported, from the strongest source down.

Ian Begley wrote that James Wiseman is a lock to make the roster. That's the clearest statement yet on the fourteenth spot. He also said there's "strong support" inside the organization for keeping Bruce Brown, and that the Knicks would "explore all avenues" to make it happen. Then the key line: "The Knicks would need to make a trade by Monday afternoon if they want to keep Brown." Begley said it's logical to assume the Knicks will try to move Pacôme Dadiet, but that they'd also explore other deals, including ones involving Tyler Kolek, over the weekend and on Monday.

He added the price. The Knicks will be asking teams to take on a player without sending salary back, and to get that done, they may need to attach second-round picks. The Knicks have eleven second-round picks over the next seven drafts, so they have the currency.

Brett Siegel of ClutchPoints reported that the Knicks have moved past simply gauging Dadiet's value and into active discussions with potential suitors, with at least two teams. Nothing finalized.

And Stefan Bondy of the Post named Kolek and Dadiet as the two most logical trade pieces. He noted that both have team options for next season and that neither has made progress toward an extension.

So that's the real story. Not "the Knicks are trading for a star." The Knicks are trying to give away a small salary, probably with a second-round pick attached, before Monday's roster deadline, so they can sign both Wiseman and Bruce Brown and still stay under the second apron.

Why Monday? Because every team has to cut down to its regular-season roster before the season opens. The Knicks open on Tuesday at the Garden against Philadelphia, ring night and banner night. So Monday is the last day to make this work before they'd have to waive Bruce Brown.

Now let's do the arithmetic, quickly, because we've built it up over the last few videos.

The Knicks have thirteen guaranteed contracts. They're about three point two seven million dollars under the second apron, and ownership treats that line as a hard ceiling. A one-year veteran minimum counts about two point four five million against the apron. One fits. Two would be about four point nine million, which doesn't. So Wiseman fits, and Bruce Brown doesn't, unless salary goes out.

Option one: trade Dadiet, who makes about two point nine eight million. The cushion grows to about six point two five million. Two veteran minimums at four point nine fit, with about one point three five million to spare.

Option two: trade Kolek, who makes about two point three million this season, about seven hundred thousand less than Dadiet. The cushion grows to about five point five seven million. Two minimums still fit, but with only about six hundred and seventy thousand to spare.

Either one works. In both cases, the Knicks go from thirteen guaranteed players to twelve, add Wiseman and Bruce Brown, and end up at fourteen. And remember, that still means no fifteenth man. This trade doesn't open a spot for anybody else. It decides who's in the fourteenth.

Now, why is something this small so hard to do? There are three reasons, and they matter.

First, the receiving team needs room. To take Dadiet or Kolek without sending salary back, a team has to be under the cap by enough to absorb the contract, or have a trade exception big enough. Most teams don't have either.

Second, and this is the part almost nobody talks about, the receiving team also needs an open roster spot, and it needs it on the exact weekend when every team in the league is cutting players to get down to fifteen. Imagine you're a general manager with fifteen guaranteed contracts. Taking on Dadiet means you have to waive someone else, which might mean eating a guaranteed salary. So you'd want a sweetener to make that worthwhile. That's why the Knicks would need to attach second-round picks.

Third, the Knicks aren't the only team doing this. Other teams around the league are also trying to attach second-round picks to get through their own roster crunches this weekend. So it's a buyer's market for cap space. The teams with room can be picky.

There's a tool that helps here, and it's worth explaining. A trade exception. When a team sends out more salary than it takes back in a trade, it can get an exception worth the difference, and for up to a year it can use that exception to absorb a player without sending salary back. So the realistic partners for a Dadiet or Kolek deal are teams under the cap, or teams holding an exception big enough to fit about two to three million dollars, and with a roster spot to spare. That's a short list on any weekend. On cutdown weekend, it's even shorter.

And second-round picks are easier to trade than firsts. The rule that stops teams from trading their first-round picks in back-to-back future drafts, the Stepien rule, only applies to firsts. Seconds can be moved freely. That's why they're the standard currency for small deals like this.

That's why the Knicks' eleven second-round picks matter. They give the front office the ability to outbid other teams for the few spots available. It's not glamorous. But it's how championship rosters stay together under the new rules.

Now, the big question. Dadiet or Kolek? Let me make the case for each, because this decision says a lot about what the Knicks value.

Start with Pacôme Dadiet. He's twenty-one, a six-nine French wing, taken twenty-fifth overall in the 2024 draft. He's in his third season. Over forty-seven career NBA games, he's averaged about one point seven points and one rebound, and he barely played in the playoffs. In the preseason, he was more involved, about seven points in fifteen minutes, but he shot only about thirty-five percent from the field and thirty-three from three. He had ten points in the finale against Toronto, but he went one for seven from deep. He's long, athletic and still raw. And the Knicks have a decision due by October 31st on his fourth-year option for 2027-28. And that option is actually a small selling point. A team that trades for Dadiet now gets about two weeks to look at him in practice before deciding whether to pick up his option. If they decline it, he's a one-year, three-million-dollar flyer with no long-term commitment. If they like him, they have cheap control for another season. For a rebuilding team with a roster spot, that's not nothing.

The case for trading Dadiet is that he's furthest from the rotation. On a team chasing a title this year, he's a development project who probably won't play meaningful minutes unless there are a lot of injuries. And the Knicks have been shopping him for weeks, which tells you how they see him.

But here's the problem. That shopping has been going on for weeks, and nothing has happened. James L. Edwards the third of The Athletic reported earlier this week that there hasn't been real interest. Some fans have said Dadiet has no value at all. I wouldn't go that far. A twenty-one-year-old former first-round pick with his size always has some value to a rebuilding team. But his value is low right now, and that's why the Knicks have had to widen the search.

Now Tyler Kolek. He's twenty-five, a six-two point guard, taken forty-fourth overall in the 2024 draft. The Knicks traded three second-round picks to move up and get him, which tells you how much they liked him. In his first two seasons, he's played a hundred and three games, averaging about three and a half points and two assists. He was in the rotation for stretches of last season, then dropped out when Jose Alvarado took those minutes. This preseason, he's been inconsistent, shooting about thirty-six percent. But he had one of the best nights of any reserve in the opener in Philadelphia, and he plays the way Mike Brown wants. He organizes, he takes contact, and his defense has improved every year. He has about two years and four point seven eight million dollars left on his deal, and as Bondy noted, next season is a team option, so whoever has him controls his future cheaply.

The case for trading Kolek is the opposite of Dadiet. He has more value around the league, so a deal is more likely. Other teams see him as a real backup point guard. And on this roster, he's the third point guard, behind Brunson and with McBride and Alvarado also in the guard rotation. His skill set is the most duplicated on the team.

The case against trading Kolek is that he's a better player right now, and he's cheap. If Brunson misses time this season, Kolek is the closest thing the Knicks have to a backup organizer. McBride is a combo guard and a shooter. Alvarado is a pest and a spark. Kolek is a point guard. And the Knicks would be giving away a player they paid three second-round picks for, possibly with more seconds attached. That's a lot of draft capital spent on one player who's then given away.

So my view? If the Knicks can move Dadiet for a second-round pick, they should, and they should do it today. If the only way to keep Bruce Brown is to move Kolek, I'd think hard about it. Here's the question I'd ask: who's more likely to matter in a playoff series in May, Bruce Brown or Tyler Kolek? My answer is Bruce Brown. He's a champion who played all eighty-two games last season, defends multiple positions, and brings the physical edge Mike Brown has been asking for all preseason. Kolek is a nice player on a cheap contract, but he's the fourth guard on a title team. So if I'm the Knicks, and it comes down to Kolek or Bruce Brown, I make the trade. But I'd do it reluctantly. Kolek has earned his place in this league, and whichever team gets him will be getting a real backup point guard.

Let me give you the counterargument, because it's a good one. The Knicks' bench defense was the biggest problem all preseason. But is Bruce Brown, at thirty, really the answer, or is it the guys already in the rotation, McBride, Alvarado, Shamet and Clarkson, who need to defend better? You could argue that adding Brown is a patch, and that Kolek's development has more long-term value. If you believe the Knicks' window is longer than this season, keeping Kolek makes sense. That's reasonable. I just think the title window is now.

And let me steelman the option nobody's talking about: doing nothing. Keep Dadiet and Kolek, sign Wiseman, and let Bruce Brown go. The argument is that the Knicks already have a deep guard and wing rotation, that Brown would rarely play when everyone's healthy, and that young, cheap players are the only kind of asset a second-apron team can keep adding to. Every second-round pick you give away now is one you can't use at the deadline to make a bigger move. If you think the Knicks' real upgrade will come in February, saving the picks makes sense. I don't agree, because I think depth problems show up long before February, but it's a fair position.

What about Deuce McBride? His name came up too, because he's on an expiring, very team-friendly deal, and there's been talk that he could be moved at some point this season. But a McBride trade is a completely different kind of deal. He's a rotation player, and the Knicks wouldn't move him just to clear a roster spot. They'd want a real player back, maybe a younger big or a wing. That kind of deal is much more likely near the February deadline, not this weekend. Remember, players who signed this summer, like Clarkson and Drummond, can't be traded until December 15th, which gives the Knicks more ways to match salary later in the season. For now, McBride stays.

And one honest caveat. Posting and Toasting published a piece arguing the Knicks don't strictly have to trade anyone to keep both players. I wasn't able to read the full argument, so I won't present it as fact. But I'll say this: every major reporter covering the team, Begley, Bondy, Siegel, Edwards, is describing a trade as the path. So that's the scenario I'm working from.

So if a trade happens, here's what the opening-night roster would probably look like. The starters: Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, Karl-Anthony Towns and Josh Hart. The guards behind them: Deuce McBride, Jose Alvarado, Landry Shamet and Jordan Clarkson. On the wing, Mohamed Diawara and Bruce Brown. At center, Andre Drummond and James Wiseman. And then whichever of Kolek or Dadiet stays. That's fourteen. Plus the three two-way players, Kevin McCullar, Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins, who can be active for up to fifty regular-season games each.

So what does this whole saga tell you about this front office?

It tells you the second apron is truly a hard line for ownership. A lot of teams say that and then cross it when it matters. The Knicks, at least so far, haven't. They let Mitchell Robinson walk to Boston. They signed Drummond and Clarkson at minimum numbers. They paused every extension negotiation. And now they're willing to give away a young player, maybe with draft picks attached, just to fit a fourteenth man. You can debate whether that's the right philosophy. But it's consistent. And it tells you that every decision this season, every injury replacement, every trade, will go through that same filter.

It also tells you that they value Bruce Brown. You don't spend second-round picks and young players on a camp invite you don't believe in. Begley's "strong support" comment matters. The coaching staff wants him.

And one more angle, quickly, because Tuesday matters. Sportsbooks have the Knicks around a two-to-one favorite on opening night against Philadelphia. Minus two hundred implies about a sixty-seven percent chance. That sounds right to me for a home team on ring night against a Sixers team that has LeBron but is still figuring itself out. But remember what Mike Brown has been saying all preseason. The record in October doesn't matter. The habits do. A ring ceremony is emotional, and emotional nights can go either way. Watch the first six minutes. If the Knicks come out flat after the ceremony, Philadelphia is good enough to make them pay.

And there's history in this matchup. The Knicks swept Philadelphia in the second round last spring. That sweep is a big part of why the Sixers reshaped their roster, and it's why LeBron is now in Philadelphia. At media day he said he "didn't leave my family to lose in the second round." So the Sixers will come into the Garden on ring night with a point to prove, with LeBron, Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey, and with a former Knick, Ariel Hukporti, on their bench. The Knicks will want to answer that. But for this team, opening night is also the first real look at the rotation questions we've spent the whole preseason on. Does Wiseman play in the first half? Does Bruce Brown get minutes, if he's still here? Does Towns play any time at the four? The answers will tell you more about this season than the score.

So what should you watch over the next seventy-two hours?

One, the trade itself. If Dadiet is moved, look at what the Knicks attach. One second-round pick is a fair price. Two or more tells you the market for cap space was tight.

Two, if it's Kolek instead, look at who the Knicks are trading him to and whether they get anything back, even a future second. If a team is willing to give something for Kolek, that tells you how the league values him.

Three, the contracts. When Wiseman and Bruce Brown sign, look at whether the deals are guaranteed. Non-guaranteed deals give the Knicks a January exit. Guaranteed ones would be a big vote of confidence.

And four, if nothing happens by Monday afternoon, Bruce Brown will most likely be waived. If so, watch where he lands. My guess is he won't be unemployed for long.
