The title says the Knicks are going to land a new star. They’re not. There’s no new star, and there’s no trade. What there is, is a trade proposal from Garrett Kerman at ClutchPoints that would send Landry Shamet, James Wiseman and two second-round picks to Orlando for center Goga Bitadze. Bitadze is a useful backup big. He’s not a star. And the proposal has one big problem that nobody seems to have noticed, which we’ll get to. But first, the good news, because there’s a lot of it. The Knicks are nine and six. They’ve won six of their last eight. And they just held on to beat Minnesota on a night when Karl-Anthony Towns carried them again. So let’s go through it all: the Minnesota win, the numbers that stood out, the Bitadze idea and why it can’t happen yet, the Miles McBride question, and the buyout market.

Let’s start with Minnesota, because it was a game that showed both the best and the worst of this team. The Knicks led by eighteen in the first half and by sixteen early in the fourth quarter. Then the offense dried up. Minnesota won the fourth quarter twenty-one to twelve and cut it to a one-possession game with two minutes and thirty-four seconds left. And from there, nobody scored again. The game came down to one defensive play: Landry Shamet stealing the ball from Jonathan Kuminga in the closing seconds. That steal won the game, plain and simple.

Think about that for a second. A few days after scoring twenty-three in the overtime win in Toronto, Shamet saved the Knicks again, this time on defense. And he’s the player the trade proposal wants to send away. Keep that in mind, because it matters later.

Karl-Anthony Towns had twenty-two points and twelve rebounds. Another double-double, again. Jalen Brunson had twenty-one points and seven assists. OG Anunoby scored fifteen. Josh Hart had eleven, which is good to see after his rough stretch. And Shamet added ten. After the game, Towns said, we still have a lot of growing to do, and tomorrow’s another day for us to test ourselves and get better. That’s the right message. The Knicks won, but they know a sixteen-point fourth-quarter lead shouldn’t turn into a one-possession game.

And there’s something bigger hiding in that finish. Remember, the Knicks came into the Toronto overtime win at one and six in clutch games this season. Their only clutch win had been in Chicago. Now, in the span of a few days, they won in overtime in Toronto, and they held on in a one-possession finish in Minnesota. That’s two close wins in a row, both decided by defense and timely plays from more than one player. Brunson hit the dagger in Toronto. Shamet made the steal in Minnesota. That’s the kind of shared clutch play the Knicks were missing earlier in the season, when too many possessions ended in isolation. It’s not fixed yet. A fourth quarter with only twelve points is still a problem. But it’s progress.

It was also good to see Josh Hart score eleven. After his scoreless night against Indiana and a stretch where he hadn’t scored more than eight points in six straight games, eleven points in a road win is a step in the right direction. Hart has been dealing with a calf injury from September and a right ankle problem that’s flared up during the season. If his legs are starting to come back, that’s huge for a team that needs his energy and rebounding.

Sports Illustrated pulled out four numbers from that game, three good and one bad, and they tell the story well.

The first number is fifty. With ten minutes left, the Knicks were shooting better than fifty percent from three and from the field overall. Against Minnesota, which SI described as the best defense in the league at guarding the three-point line, that’s one of the most impressive things the Knicks have done all season. The flow slowed down late, but for most of the night, the offense was humming.

The second number is zero. OG Anunoby was the only Knick with no zeros in his stat line. He was first on the team in plus-minus, second in rebounds, second in blocks and third in scoring. That’s Anunoby. He doesn’t always show up in the headlines, but he fills every column of the box score. On a night when the Knicks needed everyone, he gave them everything.

The third number is about rebounding. The Knicks won second-chance points twelve to four. And Minnesota had been averaging about thirteen and a half offensive rebounds a game, sixth in the league. The Knicks held them to six. For a team that’s been worried about its frontcourt depth, that’s a big deal.

And the fourth number, the bad one, is eight. That’s how many minutes Miles McBride played. SI called it a continuing downward trend. He played fourteen and nine minutes in the two games against Toronto, and then just eight against Minnesota, where he scored one basket and didn’t do much else. SI even said that if Minnesota was any indication, Tyler Kolek isn’t the low man on the depth chart right now, McBride is. That’s a big change for a player who had a plus-minus of plus thirty-nine against Milwaukee just a couple of weeks ago.

Now, the trade proposal. Here’s the idea from ClutchPoints. The Knicks get Goga Bitadze from Orlando. The Magic get Landry Shamet, James Wiseman and two future second-round picks.

Let’s start with why it’s appealing. The article points to the exact problem we’ve been talking about for a month: the frontcourt behind Towns. It describes Andre Drummond as an aging veteran and Wiseman as a reclamation project, and says they’ve struggled to anchor the paint when Towns sits. That’s harsh on Drummond, who’s been better lately, but the point stands. The Knicks need more size.

So who is Bitadze? He’s twenty-seven, he’s six-eleven, and he’s on an expiring contract worth about seven point six million dollars. The video says that through eleven games, he’s averaging about four point nine points and three point nine rebounds in about twelve minutes, while shooting sixty-nine percent from the field. The article calls him a six-eleven bruiser in his prime who provides the physicality, rim protection and screening presence the Knicks lost. That’s basically the job description of Mitchell Robinson.

A little more background on Bitadze, because he’s an interesting player. He was a first-round pick of the Indiana Pacers back in twenty nineteen, a big man from the country of Georgia, and he’s been with Orlando for a few seasons now. He’s not a star, and he’s never been a big scorer. But he’s a big body who sets hard screens, rebounds, and protects the paint. Those are exactly the things the Knicks have missed since Robinson left. And he’s young enough, at twenty-seven, that he could be more than a rental if the Knicks wanted to keep him next summer.

And why would Orlando do it? Playing time. With Wendell Carter Jr. starting and Nikola Vučević back in Orlando on a one-year deal, according to the video, Bitadze has been squeezed into a small bench role ahead of free agency next summer. If the Magic aren’t going to keep him, getting a shooter, a flyer on Wiseman and two second-round picks is better than losing him for nothing.

And the money works, at least on paper. Shamet and Wiseman together make a little more than Bitadze’s seven point six million. Because the Knicks are above the first apron, they can’t take back more salary than they send out, and in this deal, they’d actually be sending out more. So the Knicks would get a center and get slightly cheaper at the same time. That’s rare for a team in the Knicks’ position, and it’s why the idea got so much attention.

But here’s the problem nobody mentioned. Shamet can’t be traded right now. He re-signed with the Knicks this summer, and because of when and how that deal was signed, he isn’t trade-eligible until January fifteenth. Wiseman also signed in the offseason, so he can’t be traded until December fifteenth. So this exact trade can’t happen until the middle of January at the earliest.

And there’s a second issue: the roster. It’s a two-for-one trade, so the Knicks would drop from fourteen standard players to thirteen. They’d have to sign someone to get back to fourteen within two weeks. But as ESPN’s Bobby Marks pointed out, the Knicks are only about eight hundred nine thousand dollars under the second apron, and they can’t fit a new minimum contract until around February fourteenth, when the prorated amount gets small enough. That means a two-for-one deal really needs to happen close to the February eleventh deadline. So even if the Knicks loved this idea, the timing would be very tight.

Now let’s talk about the basketball. This is where I land on the trade, and it’s a no, at least right now. Shamet has been the Knicks’ best bench player this season. The video says he’s averaging about ten and a half points and shooting about forty-three and a half percent from three. According to Daily Knicks, the Knicks are about plus sixteen per one hundred possessions when he’s on the floor, and he has the best on-off net rating on the team. Opponents are shooting under thirty percent from three when he’s guarding them. He signed for about twenty-four million dollars over four years, which is a bargain. And in the last week, he scored twenty-three in an overtime win and made the game-saving steal in Minnesota. Mike Brown has said it’s hard to find minutes for Bridges and even Hart because Shamet and McBride have been playing so well.

And let’s be fair to Drummond, because the article was a little harsh. He’s been better lately. Mike Brown named him the defensive player of the game in the first Cleveland win, when he held Evan Mobley to oh for three. He had fourteen rebounds in sixteen minutes against Milwaukee. And the Knicks just held Minnesota, one of the best offensive rebounding teams in the league, to six offensive boards. Drummond isn’t the perfect backup center, and SNY’s Ian Begley has said he’s not sure Drummond is the answer in April. But he’s not the disaster some people make him out to be.

So you’d be trading your best bench player, on a cheap long-term deal, for a backup center on an expiring contract who’s averaging about five points. The video’s host said he’s about forty-five percent yes and keeps moving back and forth. I understand that. The math says trade a guard for a big. But the math doesn’t account for the fact that this particular guard is one of the reasons they’re winning.

If the Knicks want Bitadze, or a player like him, I’d rather they build the deal around a different guard. And that brings us to McBride.

The McBride question is really interesting right now. On one side, his minutes are dropping. Eight minutes against Minnesota. Kolek seems to have passed him in the rotation for now. He’s on an expiring contract, and the Knicks may not be able to afford him next summer without crossing the second apron. That all points to McBride as the natural trade piece. Stefan Bondy of the New York Post called him a natural trade candidate earlier this week.

But on the other side, Daily Knicks made a smart argument for keeping him. Last season, Mitchell Robinson was on an expiring deal too. Everyone assumed he’d be traded. The Knicks got calls, and they kept him. He played a big role in the championship run, and then he left for Boston on a three-year deal worth about forty-seven million. Daily Knicks argued that wasn’t a mistake. The Knicks used an expiring contract to win a title. That’s the best possible use of it. And the article said if the Knicks believe they can make a real run as they are, it might be worth holding onto McBride and letting him choose his own destiny in the summer.

The video’s host pushed back on that, and I think he has a point. You don’t hold onto a player for a title run if he’s not in your rotation. Robinson was playing real minutes. McBride just played eight. If his minutes keep shrinking, the argument for trading him gets stronger. If they bounce back, the argument for keeping him gets stronger. The next few weeks will tell us a lot, and Brown’s rotation choices will be the best clue.

Here’s what I’d do. If the Knicks want a center like Bitadze, I’d look at a version built around McBride and a small salary instead of Shamet. McBride makes about three point nine six million. Add Pacôme Dadiet’s expiring two point nine eight million, and you’re at about six point nine four million. That’s a little short of Bitadze’s seven point six million, and above the first apron, the Knicks can’t take back more than they send. After December fifteenth, you could add or swap in Jose Alvarado, who makes about four point four million. McBride and Alvarado together are about eight point four million, which would clear Bitadze’s salary. That keeps Shamet, consolidates two guards into one big, and fixes the biggest hole on the roster. Nobody has reported anything like that, but it fits the math better and fits the basketball better.

Now, the last story: the buyout market. Daily Knicks made the case that the trade market might be very tight this season. Executives around the league are predicting a buttoned-up sellers’ market, which makes it harder for buyers to make deals. And it’s even harder for contenders like the Knicks, who don’t have expendable mid-size salaries, assets or financial flexibility to win bidding wars.

So the alternative is the buyout market, when teams release veterans in February and contenders sign them. The video pointed to last season, when it says Jeremy Sochan negotiated his way out of San Antonio on February eleventh and signed with the Knicks two days later. I’d treat that detail as the video’s version. But the general idea is real. The buyout market can help a team like the Knicks.

The video floated some names who could become available: Brook Lopez if the Clippers fall apart, DeAndre Ayton if he’s bought out and reunites with Bridges, Bitadze himself if Orlando waives him instead of trading him, Jay Huff if Indiana cuts him, and maybe Jalen Smith in Chicago. Those are all hypotheticals. None of them have been bought out.

But there’s a rule that matters a lot here. ESPN noted that the Knicks can’t sign a player who was waived with a pre-existing salary of fifteen million dollars or more. That’s the rule for teams above the first apron. The video describes it a little differently, but that’s the key number. It rules out some of the bigger names. And as Daily Knicks pointed out, waiting for the buyout market shouldn’t be the Knicks’ first plan. The pickings are usually slim, and it leaves them at the mercy of other teams’ decisions.

And let’s keep the record in perspective. Nine and six isn’t where anyone expected the defending champions to be in late November. But after starting oh and three, the Knicks have gone nine and three. They’ve won six of their last eight, and they’ve started winning close games again. The East is crowded, with Boston, Philadelphia, Detroit, Cleveland and others all in the mix, but nobody is running away with it. If the Knicks can add one smart piece at center without giving up the players who are winning them games right now, they’ll be right where they want to be by spring.

So let me give you my verdicts.

On the Bitadze trade: the idea is good. Bitadze is the kind of center the Knicks need. But this version costs them Shamet, who’s been their best bench player and just won them a game. And Shamet can’t even be traded until January fifteenth. I’d say no to this version, and yes to exploring one built around McBride and another guard instead.

On Towns: he’s been the most reliable Knick all season. Double-double after double-double, as he chases Willis Reed’s franchise record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season. Twenty-two and twelve in Minnesota. Twenty-eight and seventeen in Toronto. Twenty-nine and thirteen in Cleveland. He’s quietly become the steadiest player on the team. And every one of those games makes the trade talk about him sound a little more ridiculous.

On Anunoby: no zeros in the box score. He’s the quiet engine of this team.

On McBride: his minutes are dropping, and that makes him the most logical trade piece. But if he plays his way back into the rotation, keeping him for the playoffs could make sense, just like it did with Robinson last season.

On the buyout market: it’s a backup plan, not a first plan. Good teams don’t wait for other teams to hand them help.

And on the title: there’s no new star coming. There’s a proposal for a solid backup center, and it’s built around the wrong guard. A more honest title would be something like, would you trade Landry Shamet for Goga Bitadze? Or, the Knicks win in Minnesota, and the trade idea that costs too much.

So let me ask you. Would you trade Shamet, Wiseman and two second-round picks for Goga Bitadze? Or would you rather build a deal around McBride? And should the Knicks keep McBride for a title run, the way they kept Mitchell Robinson last season? Let me know what you think, and I’ll see you in the next one.
