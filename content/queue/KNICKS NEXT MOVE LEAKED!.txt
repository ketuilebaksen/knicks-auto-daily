Olivier Sarr.

Now, I want to be fair here. He's not his brother. He's not going to be that kind of player. But that's a completely unreasonable bar, and it shouldn't disqualify him from a camp invite. What he actually is, is a guy who can block shots and has shown real potential.

I'm not saying you give him a big contract or even a guaranteed deal. I'm saying you bring him in and find out whether he's the shot blocker you've been looking for all summer.

And here's where the mechanics get interesting, because this is the smartest part of the entire plan.

If you sign a guy like Sarr to a non-guaranteed deal, his salary does not count against the cap until January first.

Think about what that gives you. You get months of actual evaluation — real practices, real games, real NBA competition — at effectively zero long-term cost. If it works, fantastic, you found your third center for nothing. If it doesn't work, you waive him before that January first threshold and you've lost nothing at all.

And in the meantime, you're watching the season unfold. You're seeing where your trade options lie. And that cuts both directions — who should the Knicks try to trade, and who should they try to trade for. Both of those questions get clearer answers in December than they do in September.

That's why I think delaying is genuinely the best strategy here. Use young players like Sarr to buy time. Use two of those two-way spots on bigs. Build a roster that doesn't force you into a final decision right now.

Because look at what happened last winter. The Knicks found themselves in a spot where they needed to move Guerschon Yabusele and it was genuinely hard to do.

And here's the lesson nobody drew from it. The problem was the second year on his deal. A player option in year two means the team has almost no control — you can't move a guy cleanly when there's an extra season attached that only he gets to decide on. Had he been on a one-year deal, the whole situation looks different.

And honestly? The Knicks got lucky. Yabusele declined that option and went back overseas. Most players do not turn down a guaranteed second year just to find a better situation. He was a rare case, and New York got out of a bind because of a choice that had almost nothing to do with them.

That's not going to happen again. You don't build a roster hoping for luck twice.

Which is exactly why I want guys like Sarr and Xavier Tillman on non-guaranteed deals. You wait until right before that January first threshold, you look at what you actually have, and then you decide whether to keep them or move on.

That's not being passive. That's buying yourself information for free — and information is the one thing a team pressed against the apron can actually afford.
