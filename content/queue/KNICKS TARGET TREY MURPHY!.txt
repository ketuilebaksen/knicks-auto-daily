Now the arithmetic, because this is where the trade goes from unlikely to impossible.

Rule one. The Knicks are above the first apron, and they're about three point two seven million dollars under the second apron, which ownership treats as a hard ceiling. A team above the first apron can't take back more salary in a trade than it sends out. Not one dollar more.

Murphy makes twenty-seven million this season. McBride makes just under four million. So McBride for Murphy means the Knicks would be taking back about twenty-three million more than they send. It's not close. It's not a matter of adding a minimum contract. It's illegal under the rules the Knicks are operating under. The video's trade can't be processed by the league office.

So let's do the honest version. What would it actually take, in salary, for the Knicks to get Murphy?

They'd need to send out at least twenty-seven million. And remember, the players the Knicks signed as free agents this summer, Clarkson and Drummond among them, can't be traded until December 15th. But look at what happens next. You'd be sending out three players and taking back one. The roster drops from fourteen to twelve. And the league requires fourteen standard contracts during the season. Four point nine doesn't fit into four point one. So the trade that works on salary breaks the roster.

That's what nobody making these videos understands. At the second apron, a trade isn't just about matching salaries. It's about how many bodies you have afterward and whether you can afford to replace them.
