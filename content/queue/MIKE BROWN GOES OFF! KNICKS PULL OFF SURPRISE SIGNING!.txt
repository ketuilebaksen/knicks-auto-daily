Mike Brown didn't "go off," and the Knicks didn't pull off a surprise signing. What actually happened is quieter and, honestly, more interesting. The Knicks made James Wiseman's spot official, Mike Brown gave a very candid explanation of why he chose Wiseman over Bruce Brown, and the decision turned out to protect something most fans haven't thought about: the Knicks' ability to make a trade before Christmas. And on the side, there's a twist in Dallas involving a center the Knicks tried to sign this summer.

So today, three things. What Mike Brown really said. Why waiving Bruce Brown was about trade flexibility as much as roster fit, with the numbers. And the Moussa Cissé situation in Dallas, which brings up an old question about the Mavericks and the Knicks.

Welcome back to Ketuil KNICKS. One day until banner night. Let's go through it.

First, Mike Brown. Here's what he said on Sunday, and you'll see why "goes off" is the wrong description.

On Wiseman: "When you have a guy that's twenty-five years old, that has the length and the athleticism and the talent of a James Wiseman," because, in his words, "KAT's not athletic like that, and Andre was once that." And then: "How good he runs. You welcome that at his size and age and athleticism." So the coach is saying exactly what we've been saying for weeks. Towns is a skilled, stretch big. Drummond is a strong, savvy veteran who used to be an explosive athlete. Wiseman is the only center on the roster who can run the floor and play above the rim. That's a role, and it's a specific one.

On Bruce Brown, the coach was warm, not harsh. "Bruce, he's a winner," he said. "He shows a level of toughness and versatility, not just defensively. He can guard one through four, but even offensively." He explained that the Knicks play out of concepts, and their point guard doesn't have to come down and break his man down every play, so Bruce Brown was good enough to initiate the offense or play off the ball. "So he fits in with how we want to play on both ends," he said. And then: "Having a chance to get to know him, he seems like a good guy. He seems like he knows a lot of our guys because he was in the same class as a few of them. So it was tough from that standpoint."

Now, I suspect that one line is where the "goes off" headlines came from: "KAT's not athletic like that." Out of context, it sounds like the coach taking a shot at his star, in the same week Towns said he was disappointed about the paused extension talks. But read the full sentence. Brown is explaining why a third center has to be a different kind of player from the two in front of him. He's describing a skill set, not criticizing a person. Towns has never been a high flyer. He's one of the best shooting big men in the history of the game, and Brown has said this month that he has a better feel for how to use him, including a little more time at the four. If anything, adding a vertical big like Wiseman is what makes those Towns-at-the-four lineups possible. I'd be careful with any video that turns that quote into a feud.

That's not a coach going off. That's a coach explaining a hard decision with respect for the guy he had to let go. If anything, it tells you the coaching staff really did want Bruce Brown, and that the decision was about the roster math and the need for size, not about his play.

And for Bruce Brown, there's still a sliver of a path back. Earlier this week, James L. Edwards the third of The Athletic said he thought the Knicks would find a way to keep Brown "whether now or later." "Now" didn't happen. "Later" would require two things: Brown staying unsigned, and the Knicks making a trade that sends out more salary than it brings back. Neither is likely in the next few weeks. A thirty-year-old champion who can guard four positions will get calls, especially once injuries start piling up around the league in November. But if he's still available when the Knicks make a move, his relationships in that locker room and the coach's comments this weekend suggest the door isn't closed.

And to settle the second half of the title: Wiseman's spot is now official. He's on the final fourteen-man roster as the third center, after averaging eight points and a bit over four rebounds in four preseason games. That isn't a surprise signing. It's the expected one, and we've been building to it since mid-September.

Now, the part that most coverage missed. Why did keeping Dadiet and Kolek matter so much? It's about trade flexibility, and the rule behind it is one we've mentioned before.

Players who sign as free agents can't be traded until three months after they sign or until December 15th, whichever comes later. That covers everyone the Knicks signed this summer: Drummond, Clarkson, and other players who re-signed. It also now covers Wiseman, who just signed a standard deal and won't be tradable until mid-January. According to an analysis by Daily Knicks, that leaves the Knicks with very few non-starters they can trade before December 15th. Essentially Dadiet, Kolek and Deuce McBride.

Now think about what would have happened if the Knicks had traded Dadiet or Kolek to keep Bruce Brown. That small list would have shrunk to two players. And Bruce Brown himself, as a new signing, couldn't have been traded for months. So the Knicks would have gone into November and early December with almost no way to make a trade, even if an injury or a problem made one necessary.

Here's where the numbers get specific. The Knicks are above the first apron, so in any trade, they can't take back more salary than they send out. Dadiet makes about two point nine eight million. Kolek makes just under two point three. McBride makes just under four.

So if the Knicks combine McBride and Dadiet, they can take back a player making up to about six point nine four million. If they'd kept Bruce Brown instead of Dadiet, and Brown couldn't be traded, the best combination left would have been McBride plus Kolek, which only gets to about six point two five million. That gap of about seven hundred thousand dollars sounds tiny. But at the second apron, it can decide whether a trade is legal or not.

And there's a specific example that's been floated, so let me be clear about what it is. Dan Favale of Daily Knicks has suggested that, with enough draft compensation, the Knicks could pry Moussa Diabaté out of Charlotte for Dadiet. That's a writer's idea, not a report of talks. But the salary math does work, and it's a good example of why Dadiet's contract matters. Diabaté is a twenty-four-year-old big man who makes about two point four six million this season. Dadiet makes about two point nine eight. Because Dadiet's salary is higher, the Knicks could take Diabaté back without breaking the first-apron rule. Kolek's two point three million wouldn't be enough on its own. So by keeping Dadiet, the Knicks kept the one piece that could bring back a cheap young center in a one-for-one deal.

And look at how Diabaté's contract is built, because it explains a rule we keep coming back to. His deal for this season is non-guaranteed, except for two hundred fifty thousand dollars that became guaranteed on opening night. The full amount becomes guaranteed on January 10th, the league-wide guarantee date. That's the same date that will matter for Wiseman if his deal is non-guaranteed. It's also why trades for cheap role players often happen in early January. Teams decide who to keep before that date, and players on non-guaranteed deals can be moved or released before their money locks in.

Notice the irony. All week, everyone was trying to give Dadiet away. And it turns out his contract is one of the most useful pieces the Knicks have for an in-season trade. Not because he's playing well. Because his salary is the right size.

Now, does that mean a trade is coming? No. And I want to be careful here. Most teams wait until mid-December, when a much bigger group of players becomes eligible to be traded, before they make real moves. The Knicks could easily do the same. But by not trading Dadiet or Kolek last week, they kept the option of doing something earlier if they need to. If Drummond or Wiseman gets hurt in November, for example, the Knicks wouldn't be stuck for two months. That's what this decision bought them.

And this fits the front office's approach all along. They didn't announce anything about trade plans. They didn't need to. Letting a camp player go instead of selling a young asset for nothing, and keeping their in-season options open, is what a team that won in June and wants to win again would do.

Let me also give the counterargument, because it's real. Flexibility is only valuable if you use it. Bruce Brown is a proven defender who played every game last season. Dadiet barely played last year, and Kolek played eight playoff games. If the Knicks don't make a trade until February anyway, they gave up a real player in exchange for an option they never used. That's the bet. We'll find out by Christmas whether it was the right one.

Here's the calendar, so you can keep track. On December 15th, Drummond, Clarkson, Diawara and the other players who signed or re-signed this summer become tradable. Wiseman, who signed his standard deal this weekend, won't be tradable until about three months from now, around the middle of January. And the trade deadline is in early February. So the Knicks' flexibility grows in steps. A little now, with Dadiet, Kolek and McBride. A lot more in mid-December. And the most in the weeks before the deadline.

Now, the twist in Dallas. And this one has history.

This summer, the Knicks went after Moussa Cissé, a six-eleven center who played thirty-eight games as a rookie in Dallas, averaging four and a half points, nearly six rebounds and about a block a game. Cissé was a restricted free agent. The Knicks signed him to an offer sheet: two years, about four point seven million dollars total. A small, low-risk deal for a young, athletic big man who could have filled exactly the role Wiseman has now.

Cissé wasn't the only center the Knicks went after this summer, by the way. If you've been following the channel since September, you know the list. They signed Andre Drummond. They tried to trade for Yves Missi in New Orleans, and that never happened. They signed Cissé to the offer sheet. And then they brought five veterans into camp on Exhibit 9 deals, including Wiseman, Drew Eubanks and Tony Bradley. Four separate attempts to replace Mitchell Robinson before camp even opened. That's not a front office ignoring the problem. It's a front office that kept getting told no.

Dallas matched. Let me explain how that works. When a team signs a restricted free agent to an offer sheet, his current team has the right to match the exact terms and keep him. Matching is a choice, not an obligation. And when a team matches, there's an extra rule: the player can't be traded to the team that made the offer for a full year. So Dallas didn't just keep Cissé. They locked the door to New York.

Now, here's the new part. Jake Fischer of the Stein Line reported this week that Cissé's roster spot is at risk, because Dallas wants to keep veteran big man Dwight Powell on its fifteen-man roster. Marc Stein had reported earlier that the Mavericks could waive Cissé or try to consolidate players in a trade to make room for Powell. And there's a detail that makes this awkward for Dallas. Cissé's salary for this season, about two point two million dollars, became fully guaranteed when the Mavericks kept him past October 1st. So if they waive him now, they'll be paying him to leave, just a few months after going out of their way to keep him from the Knicks.

So, the uncomfortable question. Did Dallas match the offer sheet mostly to keep Cissé away from New York?

Let me give you the case for that theory first. The Mavericks already had several big men when they matched. It was never obvious how Cissé fit their rotation. And there's history. In 2022, the Knicks signed Jalen Brunson away from Dallas in free agency, which became one of the most lopsided outcomes in recent NBA history. Brunson became the face of the franchise, and this June, he was Finals MVP for a championship team. That's not something a front office forgets. So it's easy to imagine Dallas thinking: we're not losing another young player to New York.

But here's the other side, and I think it's more likely. Teams match small offer sheets all the time for basic asset reasons. A cheap, young center on a two-year deal has trade value and depth value. Dallas's roster has changed since July. Kyrie Irving is back from his ACL. Cooper Flagg is the reigning Rookie of the Year. And Masai Ujiri now runs the front office there. Roster priorities shift. Wanting to keep a veteran like Powell for his experience and locker room presence isn't proof of anything sinister. And if they waive Cissé, they'll eat the money, which is the opposite of what a team does if it's only trying to make a point. I can't tell you what Dallas was thinking in July. Nobody outside that building can. So I'd treat the "spite" theory as fun speculation, not fact.

And the honest answer for Knicks fans: even if Dallas waives Cissé today, the Knicks probably can't do anything about it.

Here's why. If he's waived, he goes on waivers for forty-eight hours, and any team can claim him by taking on his guaranteed salary of about two point two million dollars. The Knicks are at fourteen players, with Wiseman now counting against the apron. Their cushion under the second apron after signing Wiseman is only about eight hundred thousand dollars. Two point two million doesn't fit. And the champions are last in waiver priority, so other teams would get the first chance anyway. If he clears waivers and becomes a free agent, the Knicks would still need to fit his minimum salary under the apron, and they can't without moving salary first. The one-year trade restriction from the offer sheet applies to trades, not to signing him as a free agent, but the money still doesn't work.

So who would get him? Fischer reported that Philadelphia could pursue Cissé, because Ariel Hukporti, the former Knick who signed with the Sixers this summer, tore his Achilles and is out for the season. Atlanta and Chicago have also been mentioned. Wouldn't that be something? The center the Knicks tried to sign in July, ending up on the team they play on opening night, filling in for the center they lost this summer.

Dallas has to make its final decision before the regular season starts. So by the time you watch this, it may already be done. If Cissé is waived, watch where he goes. If it's Philadelphia, that's a storyline for the next time these teams meet.

So, let me pull it together.

Mike Brown didn't go off. He explained, respectfully, that the Knicks needed a young, athletic, vertical big man, and that Bruce Brown was a winner they wished they could keep. Wiseman's spot is official. Bruce Brown is a free agent. And by keeping Dadiet and Kolek, the Knicks kept a small but real ability to make a trade before December 15th, including potential one-for-one deals for a cheap young big man. Meanwhile, in Dallas, the center the Knicks tried to sign this summer might be about to become available, and the Knicks probably can't afford to grab him.
