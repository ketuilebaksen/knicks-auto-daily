Now, the roster spot itself.

Here's the situation one more time, because the arithmetic is everything. The Knicks have thirteen players on guaranteed contracts. They have two open roster spots. And Begley's line about fifteen players deserves a quick explanation, because it's a rule a lot of fans don't know. During the season, NBA teams are generally required to carry at least fourteen players on standard contracts, with only short exceptions. The Knicks have thirteen. So the fourteenth spot isn't optional. They have to fill it. The fifteenth spot is optional, and that's the one the apron takes away unless the Knicks move salary somewhere else.

That's a coherent argument. Let me state it in one sentence. If the fourteenth man is just insurance until a trade, you want the most reliable insurance, not the most exciting.

Now let me be fair to Begley on the health point, because it's real.

Wiseman has been hit hard by injuries. A knee surgery early in his career. And then a torn Achilles in twenty twenty-four, in his first game with Indiana. He's played a limited number of games in six seasons. Those aren't small things, and a team defending a title has to factor them in.

But I'd also say that the gap in risk isn't as big as the "safe play" label suggests. Eubanks has his own injury history. And Eubanks is the veteran with the lower ceiling. If he's healthy and good, he's a solid end-of-bench big. If Wiseman is healthy and good, he's a rotation player who can actually change a game. When the downside risks are closer than they look, I'd lean towards the higher upside.

And then there's the Mike Brown factor. Brown coached Wiseman at Golden State. He's been openly supportive of him. And now he's playing him while leaving the other two candidates on the bench. There's also a contract detail that I think bridges Begley's view and mine. Camp players who make the roster usually sign standard minimum contracts that don't become fully guaranteed until early January, when the league-wide guarantee date arrives. If the Knicks keep Wiseman on a non-guaranteed deal, they get to test his health and his play for the first two and a half months of the season and still have the option to waive him before his salary locks in. That gives them most of the safety Begley is asking for, while keeping the upside. I can't tell you that's how they'll structure it. But it's a common way teams handle exactly this kind of decision.

So here's where I land. I think the fourteenth spot is Wiseman's to lose. Begley's logic is sound if the Knicks are planning to trade for a centre in February and just need a safe placeholder until then. Now let's talk about the other side of the competition, because Bruce Brown made it more interesting this week.

There's something quietly important in that answer. The second apron isn't just a Knicks problem. It's reshaping the whole league. Teams are scared of it.
