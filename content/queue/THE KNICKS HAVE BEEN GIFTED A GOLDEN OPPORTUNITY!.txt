When healthy, he shot thirty-nine point seven percent from three in 2024-25 and thirty-nine point two percent in 2025-26.

Those are elite numbers for a backup wing. And they underline how much floor spacing value he brings to a team that lives off Jalen Brunson driving into the paint and kicking to shooters waiting on the perimeter. Every time Shamet is on the floor, a defense cannot sag off him or ignore him without getting punished immediately. His presence alone changes how teams structure their half-court defense against us.

The problem has been availability.

He missed thirty-two games in 2024-25 after dislocating his shoulder in the preseason — an injury that disrupted his entire first full year in this system. Then in 2025-26 he was sidelined for thirty-two more with a right shoulder sprain in the middle of the season.

Add that up and it's sixty-four regular season games without him across two years. That's a staggering number, and it prevented him from ever truly integrating into the rotation or building the kind of chemistry that only comes from playing together night after night across a full season grind.

We have genuinely never gotten to see what Landry Shamet can be over an uninterrupted stretch.

And despite those health concerns, the Knicks rewarded him with a four-year contract heading into this campaign. That's a clear signal from the front office that they believe in his value. Hart's answer was direct and exactly what you'd want to hear.

He said the Knicks aren't worried about the target, because in his view nobody is actually picking them to win again anyway. He said the team is channeling that underdog perception as fuel. And that fits a broader theme across this entire offseason. This has been a summer of players saying exactly the right things.

Those aren't empty words, because the roster backs them up. New York returns nearly its entire championship rotation — continuity that's genuinely rare in modern basketball, where trades, free agency, and injuries pull apart even the best-built teams. Intact, healthy, and determined is a rare combination for a defending champion.

The oddsmakers, though, are not convinced. DraftKings currently lists the Knicks at plus nine hundred to win the 2026-27 championship — behind both San Antonio and Oklahoma City. Some books even have Philadelphia ahead of us.

So Hart's claim that nobody is picking the Knicks? That's fully supported by where the lines sit.

Not one of the seven before us managed to even return to the Finals the following year. That's the brutal reality of a league with more parity than at any point in its history — sustaining dominance across two full seasons demands health, execution, depth, coaching, and no small amount of luck.

But the Knicks have the most underrated ingredient of all. Genuine, unified, loudly expressed belief. From Brunson setting the internal tone to Hart declaring intentions in a nationally broadcast tennis booth, this organization is sending one consistent message into camp.
