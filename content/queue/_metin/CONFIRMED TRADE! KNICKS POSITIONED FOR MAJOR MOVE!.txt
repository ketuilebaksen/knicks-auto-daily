The title says confirmed trade, and that the Knicks are positioned for a major move. Let’s be clear. There’s no confirmed trade. Nothing has happened. What actually happened is that ESPN put out a trade deadline outlook for the Knicks, with notes from Bobby Marks and other ESPN writers. It’s a really useful piece, because it lays out exactly what the Knicks can and can’t do. But it’s not a report of a deal. It’s a map, and a good one. So today, let’s walk through that map. What ESPN said about the money, what the Knicks have to trade, what position they’re most likely to target, and the one player who could be on the move if the Knicks do anything at all.

Let’s start with the big picture from ESPN. The headline idea is that the Knicks are putting off their contract issues until the offseason. That makes sense. Karl-Anthony Towns has a sixty-one million dollar player option and turned down a two-hundred-million-dollar extension offer. Josh Hart has a team option. Miles McBride is on an expiring contract. None of those decisions have to be made right now, and the rules make most of them hard to change during the season anyway. So the Knicks are waiting.

And ESPN pointed out the obvious problem: there isn’t a lot of flexibility, especially with James Dolan making it clear he won’t go into the second apron. That line has shaped everything this team has done since the summer. It’s why Mitchell Robinson left. It’s why Andre Drummond is on a minimum deal. It’s why every trade idea has to be checked against the salary rules.

ESPN also talked about Mikal Bridges. The question it asked was simple: if Bridges keeps struggling, do the Knicks start to canvass the league? But ESPN also noted the problem with that. Bridges is owed about one hundred sixteen and a half million dollars over the three seasons after this one, including a player option in twenty twenty-nine thirty. That’s a lot of money for another team to take on. And ESPN said the hope is that Bridges turns it around like he did last season, because this team needs shot creation and shot making. That’s the honest middle ground. The slump is real, but so is the contract, and so is his history of bouncing back.

Now let’s get to the most useful part: what can the Knicks actually do? Here’s what Bobby Marks laid out.

Number one: the Knicks are about eight hundred nine thousand dollars below the second apron. That’s slightly different from the number we’ve been using, which was about eight hundred twenty-four thousand. Either way, it’s tiny. The Knicks have almost no room to add salary.

Number two: because they’re above the first apron, the Knicks can’t take back more salary than they send out in a trade. That rule controls everything.

Number three: ESPN notes the Knicks can use Pacôme Dadiet’s roughly three million dollar expiring contract in a trade. Remember, the Knicks declined his option for next season at the end of October, so he’s a pure expiring deal.

Number four: ESPN says the Knicks aren’t allowed to sign a player until February fourteenth, and they can’t sign a player who was waived with a pre-existing salary of fifteen million dollars or more. That second part is the buyout rule for teams over the first apron. It means the Knicks can’t jump in and grab a big-name player who gets bought out by another team. That’s an important detail, because every year, fans dream about the buyout market. For the Knicks, the biggest names on that market will be off limits.

Let me explain why that February fourteenth date probably matters so much, because it’s the smartest detail in the whole ESPN piece. Right now, the Knicks have fourteen players and only about eight hundred nine thousand dollars of room under the second apron. Even a minimum contract costs more than that early in the season. But minimum contracts are prorated. The later in the season you sign a player, the less he counts. By the middle of February, a prorated veteran minimum gets small enough to fit under that tiny cushion. That’s most likely why the Knicks can’t add anyone until then. And it has a big effect on trades. If the Knicks trade two players for one, they drop to thirteen. The league only lets a team stay below fourteen for two weeks at a time. So a two-for-one deal basically has to happen close to the February eleventh deadline, so the Knicks can sign someone a few days later. That’s the kind of timing detail that decides how and when this front office makes a move.

And number five: ESPN says the Knicks have one first-round pick and twelve second-round picks available to trade. The one first-round pick is in twenty thirty-three, because the Knicks owe their twenty twenty-seven, twenty twenty-nine and twenty thirty-one firsts to Brooklyn from the Bridges trade. The twelve second-round picks are the real currency. That’s a lot of seconds, and in a league where teams love collecting picks, they can matter. A rebuilding team might take three or four seconds for a backup big it doesn’t really need. And if the Knicks have to add a little sweetener to make a deal work under the apron, seconds are the easiest thing to give, because they don’t count against the cap.

ESPN also pointed out something encouraging. The Knicks faced the same kind of limits last season, and they still made a deal at the deadline. That was the trade for Jose Alvarado, when Miles McBride needed core muscle surgery. Alvarado ended up playing real minutes in the Finals. So the Knicks know how to make small, smart moves under tight rules.

Now, what position are the Knicks looking at? ESPN’s answer is the same answer we’ve been giving on this channel for weeks: center. ESPN said the Knicks had, by definition, a championship-level roster last season, and the only player they lost was Mitchell Robinson. It said Andre Drummond has been a serviceable replacement so far, and that it’s hard to imagine the Knicks finding many better options on the trade market. But if one does come up, ESPN said, that’s the clearest spot on the roster worth improving.

And I agree with that, but let me give Drummond his credit too. He’s been much better lately. Mike Brown named him the defensive player of the game in the first Cleveland win, when he had eight rebounds in fourteen minutes and held Evan Mobley to oh for three. Then against Milwaukee, he grabbed fourteen rebounds in sixteen minutes. His rebounding has been phenomenal. The question is whether he can be the backup center in a playoff series against teams with elite size, like Boston, Cleveland, Oklahoma City or San Antonio. And there are some limits. On back-to-backs, it’s hard for him to play big minutes. James Wiseman is fast and athletic, but we haven’t seen enough of him to know what he can do. When Towns sits, the Knicks get the rebounding, but they lose a lot of the offense and the shot blocking at the position. That’s the gap, and it’s the gap every Knicks fan has been talking about since July.

So here’s where the original video makes its biggest prediction, and I think it’s a smart one. The host says none of the Knicks’ starters will be moved at the deadline, including Bridges. He says if the Knicks ever break up the starting group, it’ll happen in the offseason, not now. I agree. Trading a starter in the middle of a title defense, with this many rules in the way, would be a huge gamble. The deadline is February eleventh. If the Knicks do something, it’ll probably be smaller.

And that brings us to the player most likely to be moved: Miles McBride.

Let me be clear, this isn’t a report. Nobody has said the Knicks are shopping McBride. But the logic is easy to follow. McBride is on an expiring contract worth about four million dollars. He’s going to be an unrestricted free agent next summer unless the Knicks extend him. His agent, Mark Bartelstein, said in October that there may be opportunities during the season. And Josh Hart said at media day that he didn’t think an extension would get done, and that things could change by February.

So the Knicks have a choice. Keep McBride for the title run and risk losing him for nothing in free agency, the way they lost Mitchell Robinson. Or use him as a trade piece to bring in the center they need. The host points out that the Knicks seem to be featuring McBride more lately, and that’s true. He’s been hitting threes, he’s been great on defense, and he had a plus-minus of plus thirty-nine against Milwaukee. Mike Brown even said recently that it’s hard to find minutes for Bridges, and even Hart at times, because Shamet and McBride are playing so well. If McBride keeps playing like this, his trade value goes up. That’s a smart thing for a front office to watch.

One small note on the Mitchell Robinson comparison, because the video calls it a mistake. I’d put it a little differently. Robinson left because the Knicks couldn’t pay him what Boston paid, three years and about forty-seven million, without breaking Dolan’s second-apron rule. That wasn’t really a mistake. It was a choice the rules forced on them. But the lesson is the same. When a team can’t afford to keep a player, it’s usually better to get something for him before he leaves. That’s why McBride’s situation matters so much.

Now let’s do the math, because this is where it gets real. McBride makes about three point nine six million. Dadiet makes about two point nine eight million. Together, that’s about six point nine four million dollars. Because the Knicks are above the first apron, the player coming back can’t make more than what they send out, plus the small room they have under the second apron. And if they trade two players for one, they’d have to fill the open roster spot with a minimum contract. With a veteran minimum, the incoming player could make up to about five point three million. With a cheaper rookie minimum, up to about six point four million. So McBride and Dadiet together could bring back a center making somewhere around five to six million dollars. That’s not a star. But it could be a solid backup big who protects the rim.

On December fifteenth, more options open up. That’s when Jose Alvarado, Jordan Clarkson, Mohamed Diawara and Andre Drummond all become trade-eligible. Alvarado makes about four point four million. If you combined Alvarado and McBride, you’d have about eight point four million in salary, and the incoming player could make up to about six point eight million with a veteran minimum refill. That gives the Knicks a little more room to find the right big. And those twelve second-round picks could help sweeten any deal.

But here’s the catch the host mentions too. The Knicks spent the summer looking for a young, up-and-coming center who could grow with the team. Even after they signed Drummond, that was the player they wanted. And young centers on cheap deals are exactly the kind of players their current teams don’t want to give up. So would McBride, Dadiet and a couple of second-round picks be enough? Maybe not. It depends on who’s available, and right now, nobody knows.

So who could the target be? I want to be careful here, because nobody has reported any talks. But columnists have floated a few names over the past month, and some of them actually fit the math. Charles Bassey of the Warriors has a cap number of about two point four five million, exactly the same as Drummond’s, so a one-for-one swap would match to the dollar. The Clippers have two young six-eleven bigs on cheap deals, Yanic Konan Niederhäuser and Baba Miller, and Dadiet’s expiring contract could match one of them. Moussa Diabaté in Charlotte and Dylan Cardwell in Sacramento have come up too. None of those players are stars. But that’s the point. The Knicks don’t need a star at backup center. They need someone who can protect the rim for fifteen minutes a night and fit under the rules. The bigger names, the ones that come with forty or fifty million dollar contracts, just don’t work for this roster.

And if the right center isn’t out there, the host suggests the Knicks might look for a wing instead. That’s possible, but I’d be careful. The Knicks already have a crowded group of guards and wings. Brunson, Hart, Anunoby, Bridges, Shamet, McBride, Kolek, Alvarado, Clarkson, Diawara. Adding another wing doesn’t fix the biggest hole. If the Knicks make a move, it should be for size.

And let’s think about this from McBride’s side too. He’s twenty-six. He came back from core muscle surgery during last season’s title run. He’s been one of the best two-way guards off any bench in the league. And he’s playing for his next contract. If the Knicks can’t pay him what he’s worth next summer without crossing the second apron, then moving him for real help at a position of need isn’t disrespect. It’s just the reality of how this team is built. I’d hate to see him go. He’s exactly the kind of player fans love. But I’d rather the Knicks get something for him than lose him for nothing in July.

Now let’s quickly talk about tonight, because the original video was recorded before the game. The Knicks play the Indiana Pacers in an NBA Cup game. The Cup matters for this team. The Knicks won the Cup in twenty twenty-five, and they already lost their Cup opener to Philadelphia this season, ninety-eight to ninety-three. So every Cup game counts. And the video says Indiana is expected to be short-handed. After that, the Knicks play the Toronto Raptors on Sunday.

The host also had a few rotation wishes. He wants to see more of Jordan Clarkson, who didn’t play in the last Cleveland game. That’s fair. Clarkson was one of the best stories of the early season. Mike Brown called him the definition of an ultimate pro, and he scored eighteen in the comeback in Chicago. The host also wants to see Brunson and Kolek play together more. That’s an interesting idea. Kolek has been great as a playmaker, and pairing him with Brunson would let Brunson play more off the ball. We’ve only seen it briefly so far.

On the Brunson and Kolek idea, I’d add one more thing. When Kolek runs the offense, Brunson can come off screens and attack a defense that’s already moving, instead of bringing the ball up against a set defense every time. That can save Brunson’s legs over a long season. It also makes the Knicks harder to scout, because the ball isn’t always in the same player’s hands. It might not be a lineup for every game, but it’s worth trying in the regular season, when there’s time to experiment.

And the host is right about the big picture. The regular season is when you work out the kinks. The Knicks are six and five. They’ve won six of their last eight. Brunson has been on fire. Towns is chasing a Willis Reed record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season. The bench has been one of the best in the league. This is a team that should be in the mix in May and June. The question is whether one smart deadline move can make it even more dangerous.

And what if the Knicks just stand pat? Honestly, that’s a perfectly reasonable outcome too. This team won a title last June with almost the same roster. It’s won six of its last eight games. The bench has been one of the best in the league, and Drummond has been better lately. Keeping McBride for the playoff run has real value, because he’s one of the few guards on the roster who can defend the other team’s best ball handler for long stretches. If the Knicks can’t find a center who’s clearly better than what they have, the smart move might be no move at all. A front office’s job isn’t to make a trade. It’s to make the team better. Sometimes the best way to do that is to wait. Patience isn’t exciting, and it doesn’t make headlines. But it’s won this franchise a lot more than panic ever has.

So let’s put it all together.

Is there a confirmed trade? No. ESPN published a deadline outlook, not a deal.

What can the Knicks do? Not much, but something. They’re about eight hundred nine thousand dollars under the second apron. They can’t take back more salary than they send out. They can’t sign big-name buyout players. They have one first-round pick in twenty thirty-three and twelve second-round picks.

What position would they target? Center. ESPN says that’s the clearest spot to improve.

Who’s most likely to be moved? If anyone, it’s Miles McBride, probably with Dadiet, because McBride is on an expiring deal and might be hard to keep next summer. But nothing has been reported.

Will a starter be traded? Probably not before the deadline. Any big change to the starting group is more likely in the offseason.

A more honest title would be something like, ESPN’s Knicks deadline map: what they can and can’t do. Or, why Miles McBride could be the Knicks’ biggest trade chip.
