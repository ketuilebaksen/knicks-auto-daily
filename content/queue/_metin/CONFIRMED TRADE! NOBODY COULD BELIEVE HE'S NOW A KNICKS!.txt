Now let me give you the specific basketball case, because the metaphor is fun but substance is what matters.

The Knicks won a championship with Karl-Anthony Towns as the primary center — a player whose offensive gifts are genuinely historic for a big man, but whose defensive limitations have been documented his entire career.

Towns does things offensively that almost no center in NBA history has done consistently. The range, the footwork, the passing, the post game.

But in the playoffs, when opposing staffs had weeks to prepare and the intensity climbed to a level the regular season simply doesn't replicate, this team had to make real adjustments to protect him. Rotating help coverage. Switching him off the most dangerous scorers. Managing his minutes carefully in fourth quarters when specific matchups threatened to become problems.

Those adjustments worked last season. Barely. Because this roster had enough collective defensive intelligence to compensate.

But the NBA is a copycat league. Those adjustments are on film now. They've been studied. And every team we face next spring will replicate them with more precision.

Missi behind Towns changes that calculus entirely.

When Towns needs rest, or when a matchup makes it advantageous to have a pure rim protector on the floor instead of a stretch five, Missi is exactly that option. He protects the paint at an elite rate. He communicates coverages with a clarity that makes the whole unit more coherent. And his ability to switch onto perimeter players without getting completely lost is better than his age suggests.

We don't need him to be the best player on the floor. We need him to be a real NBA center who makes the defense better when he's out there and doesn't become a target opponents immediately hunt.

He clears that bar easily.

That said, here's the counterargument New Orleans genuinely should consider.

Miles McBride fits their timeline. He's twenty-five. He's a capable NBA guard with real defensive instincts and a developing offensive game. And he's on an expiring contract, which means they get a full season to evaluate him before deciding anything.

That last part matters more than people realize for a rebuilding team. An expiring deal isn't a liability — it's a free look. You take a real rotation player, you watch him for eighty-two games, and if he fits your timeline you extend him. If he doesn't, the money comes off your books entirely.

They're not looking for stars in this deal. They're looking for pieces that fit a rebuild. And adding a future second-rounder on top gives them a draft asset alongside a real player.

Now, here's the developmental angle that makes this most interesting to me.

What does Missi become with Karl-Anthony Towns as his daily mentor?

Because whatever you think about Towns defensively, his offensive knowledge and footwork are priceless for a twenty-two year old center still building that side of his game. Missi right now is a defensive specialist who contributes offensively in limited ways — mostly lobs and putbacks.

Spend a season watching Towns operate. Learning from him in film sessions. Absorbing the habits of an All-Star offensive center. Missi's offensive ceiling after three or four years of that education could be dramatically higher than his current numbers suggest.

And a center who's elite defensively at twenty-two and develops real offensive tools by twenty-five? That's a franchise-level piece.
