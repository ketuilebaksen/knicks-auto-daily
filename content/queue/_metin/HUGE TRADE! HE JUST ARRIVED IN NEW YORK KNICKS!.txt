Alright, trade wire. And we're opening with a decision that defines championship-era management.

Karl-Anthony Towns is eligible for an extension. And Stefan Bondy just reported the Knicks are unlikely to extend him before the season begins.

Here's the situation. Towns' maximum extension is four years and two hundred and seventy-two million dollars.

That is a brutal number. You cannot casually write that check and move on. There's also a lower possibility somewhere in the hundred and eighty million range — but even that is a massive commitment for a big man who'll be thirty-three by the end of a four-year deal.

And there's a real irony in it. We just won a championship with Towns as a crucial part of the puzzle. And now we have to sit back and ask whether we want to pay two hundred and seventy-two million to keep him.

Here's the real analysis, and Empire Sports Media had the right take on this. Don't panic. Don't force an answer before training camp.

The Knicks have time. Towns remains under contract. Let the season breathe. Let this front office actually watch how the group defends its title before committing to something this size.

Think about it honestly. That championship was magical because the team was healthy, locked in, and executing at an elite level. But what if Towns has a down year? What if he gets injured? What if we discover that two hundred and seventy-two million is better spent somewhere else entirely?

Because the championship discount is a real phenomenon. Some guys win rings and immediately reset their expectations downward. Others win rings and demand maximum value precisely because they proved their worth.

Towns is firmly in that second camp. He helped bring New York a title. He knows he can ask for top dollar, and honestly, he's earned the right to ask.

But we can't be emotional about this. The cap situation is already a bind. We're already stressed about the second apron. A two hundred and seventy-two million dollar extension doesn't solve problems — it creates them.

My honest, unbiased verdict? Let this breathe. There's no rush. Towns isn't going anywhere. Watch the season unfold, see how the group plays together, then make the call. Do not let a championship hangover force you into a deal that haunts you in three years.

So the real question is this. Is Miles McBride part of the long-term blueprint, or is he the player who brings back the next championship piece?

But if you extend him now, you've made your decision. He's a Knick for the next three to five years, and you've spent money you might need elsewhere.

My honest, unbiased verdict? Don't extend McBride yet. Not because he's bad — he's genuinely good. But because we need the flexibility.
