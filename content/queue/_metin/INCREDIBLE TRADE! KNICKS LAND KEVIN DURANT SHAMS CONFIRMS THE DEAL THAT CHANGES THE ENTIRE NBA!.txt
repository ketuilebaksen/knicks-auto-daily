So where did this come from? It isn't completely out of nowhere, and I think that's why it spread so fast.

If you search "Kevin Durant Knicks," you'll find articles. Heavy, Sports Illustrated's team sites, and Yahoo have all run pieces over the past year with headlines like "Rockets proposed to trade Kevin Durant to Knicks." Some of them even include OG Anunoby.

But read the word carefully. Proposed. Those are writers building hypothetical trades. They're thought experiments, not reports. Nobody says the teams are talking.

What this video did was take a proposed trade, attach Shams' name to it, add a dramatic ESPN scene, and present it as done. That jump — from "someone proposed" to "Shams confirmed" — is the whole trick.

Before we go, here's a quick checklist you can use the next time a "done deal" video shows up in your feed.

One. Check the insider's own account. If Shams, Begley, or Bondy really reported it, it's on their feed. If it isn't there, it didn't happen.

Two. Look for a second source. Real big trades get confirmed by other reporters within minutes. If nobody else is saying it, be suspicious.

Three. Check the easy facts. The coach, the record, the stats. Fake videos get those wrong all the time, because they're made fast and from templates.

Four. Watch the wording. "Proposed," "could," and "should" are opinions. "Agreed," "finalized," and "per sources" are reports. A lot of misleading videos quietly swap one for the other.
