Should the Knicks want him?

I think the honest answer is yes, in principle. And here's why.

Look at what this roster lost. Mitchell Robinson went to Boston. Robinson was the team's rim protector and offensive rebounder, a player who changed shots just by standing in the paint. The Knicks replaced him with Andre Drummond, who is a great rebounder but isn't a shot-blocker at this stage of his career. And Karl-Anthony Towns is the best shooting big in the league, but he's not a traditional rim protector either.

So there's a specific hole. Rim protection, lob finishing, vertical spacing. That's exactly what Gafford does. Almost two blocks a game two seasons ago. Seventy percent shooting, almost all of it at the rim. A player who doesn't need the ball to be effective, and who makes a guard's life easier by giving him a target above the rim.

And picture the fit with Brunson. Brunson draws two defenders in the pick and roll. Right now, the roll man is Towns, who pops to the three-point line, or Drummond, who rolls but doesn't threaten above the rim. Gafford would give the Knicks a third look: the hard roll and the lob. That forces the help defender to choose between the lob and the corner shooter. It's a real addition, and it's a different kind of addition from anything else on the roster.

There's also a defensive argument. The Knicks' perimeter defence is elite. Bridges, Anunoby and Hart can guard almost anyone. But when a guard gets past them, there's no one at the rim who scares people. Gafford would be that player for twenty minutes a night. That's what Robinson did for years.

So I'm with the video on one thing. If you're looking at the profile, Gafford makes sense. More sense, frankly, than some of the stars we've been fact-checking.
