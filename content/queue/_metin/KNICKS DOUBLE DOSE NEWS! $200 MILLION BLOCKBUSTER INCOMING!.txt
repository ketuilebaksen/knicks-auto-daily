The title promises a double dose of news and a two hundred million dollar blockbuster. There’s no blockbuster. There’s no trade, and there’s no new two hundred million dollar anything. The only two hundred million dollar number around this team is still the extension offer Karl-Anthony Towns turned down this summer. But the real news from this weekend is better than any fake trade, because the Knicks finally won a close game. On Sunday night in Toronto, they came back from thirteen points down in the fourth quarter and beat the Raptors in overtime, one twelve to one-oh-eight. That snapped a two-game losing streak and moved them to seven and six. So let’s talk about what really happened. How they finally closed a game, why the clutch numbers had been so shocking, and why Landry Shamet might be the most important role player on this roster right now.

Let’s start with the game. The Knicks were in trouble. Down thirteen in the fourth quarter, on the road, after losing to Cleveland and Indiana. A third straight loss would have dropped them below five hundred again. And they’d just watched the end of regulation go wrong too. They had a chance to win it in the final possession of the fourth quarter, it didn’t work, and then Scottie Barnes hit a step-back three to help force overtime. That’s the kind of moment that has broken this team in close games all season.

But this time, they didn’t break. In overtime, they outscored Toronto seventeen to thirteen. With the score tied at one-oh-six and thirty-three seconds left, Jalen Brunson rose up from the corner and buried a three that gave the Knicks the lead for good. Then OG Anunoby, playing against his former team, sealed it at the free-throw line. Anunoby finished with twenty-one points and nine rebounds.

And there’s a great storyline inside that ending. Anunoby came to the Knicks from Toronto in December of twenty twenty-three, in the trade that sent RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley to the Raptors. On Sunday, Quickley led the Raptors with seventeen points. And Anunoby was the one at the free-throw line closing the door on his old team. That trade changed the Knicks. It gave them the wing defender who helped them win a title. And it’s also the reason that fourteen-game winning streak against Toronto goes all the way back to that same month. Since that trade, the Knicks haven’t lost to the Raptors once.

Brunson finished with twenty-eight points and nine assists, in a season-high forty-one minutes. His shooting wasn’t great. He went nine of twenty-two. But that didn’t matter at the end. After the failed possession in regulation, he could have let it get to him. Instead, he made sure he had the last word.

And Karl-Anthony Towns matched Brunson with twenty-eight points, and added seventeen rebounds. Seventeen. On a night when the Knicks needed every extra possession, Towns controlled the glass. Another double-double, as he keeps chasing Willis Reed’s franchise record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season. And remember, this is the player some videos keep saying the Knicks should trade or replace. Twenty-eight and seventeen in a must-win road game is the best answer to that.

Think about what seventeen rebounds means in a game like that. The Knicks were cold from three in the second half. When you’re missing shots, the only way to survive is to get the ball back. Towns did that over and over. And with Josh Hart still working his way back to full strength after his ankle problems, the Knicks need Towns to be a monster on the glass. On Sunday, he was. And let’s not forget, Towns has been doing this all season. Twenty-seven and twenty against Detroit. Twenty-nine and thirteen in Cleveland. Twenty-eight and seventeen in Toronto. That’s not a player you’re looking to move. That’s a player you’re looking to pay.

Now, why did this win feel so big? Because of the clutch numbers. Coming into Sunday, the Knicks were one and six in clutch games this season. Their only clutch win had come against Chicago. Stefan Bondy of the New York Post pointed out that their net rating in those situations was one of the worst in the league among teams with a similar number of close games. Posting and Toasting had the Knicks around minus seventeen in clutch net rating, with a true shooting percentage around fifty point seven, near the bottom of the league.

That’s shocking for this team, because last season, closing games was their identity. According to the video, the Knicks won about fifty-eight and a half percent of their clutch games last regular season, seventh in the league. Brunson led the league in clutch points for the third straight year. And in the playoffs, he got even better. He led the NBA in fourth-quarter scoring in the postseason, averaging about nine point nine points in the final quarter. NBA writer Tommy Beer pointed out that the only other player in the play-by-play era to average that many fourth-quarter points on a title team was Dirk Nowitzki in twenty eleven. That’s the company Brunson was in.

And the whole team was built on refusing to go away. On the way to the franchise’s first title in more than fifty years, the video says the Knicks went six and two in playoff games when trailing by double digits, the best such record in the play-by-play era. In the Finals against San Antonio, the video says they trailed by double digits in every game and still came back. I’d treat some of those exact numbers as the video’s version, but the point is right. This team’s DNA was coming back and closing. And according to NBA dot com’s John Schuhmann, last regular season under Mike Brown, the Knicks were forty-six and six when leading by double digits, and fourteen and twenty-six when trailing by double digits, which still ranked in the top ten.

So when the Knicks fell to one and six in clutch games this season, it wasn’t just bad luck. It felt like they’d lost a part of who they were. That’s why Sunday mattered. Down thirteen in the fourth, on the road, after a heartbreaking end to regulation, they found the old version of themselves.

So what was different this time? A few things. First, the Knicks didn’t panic when regulation slipped away. After Barnes’ three, it would have been easy to let the frustration take over. Instead, they came out of the gate in overtime with Shamet’s three, and they played from in front the whole extra period. Second, they had more than one player making plays. Shamet scored seven in overtime. Brunson hit the dagger. Anunoby made his free throws. That’s three different players delivering in the clutch, which is exactly what the Knicks were missing in those one-possession losses. And third, they kept fighting on the glass. When you’re getting extra possessions, you don’t need every shot to go in. That’s how you survive a cold night.

And this connects to the biggest criticism of the Knicks’ late-game offense this season. Posting and Toasting argued that the Knicks have relied on too much isolation in close games, and that they should use Towns’ passing more to free up shooters. In overtime on Sunday, the ball found Shamet early. That’s the kind of late-game offense that takes pressure off Brunson. If the Knicks can keep doing that, the clutch numbers will start to look a lot more like last season’s.

Let’s be fair, though. The win wasn’t perfect. The Knicks shot two of ten from three in the fourth quarter and one of seven from three in the third. The offense stalled for long stretches, and they leaned heavily on Brunson to bail them out again. And Toronto was short-handed. The Raptors, now five and eight, were without Kawhi Leonard, who’s dealing with a swollen left knee. They’ve gone three and eight in the games Leonard has missed. Scottie Barnes was back after missing four games with a strained groin, and Immanuel Quickley led the Raptors with seventeen. So this was a win the Knicks needed, but it’s not a statement win.

There’s also a fun streak attached to it. The Knicks have now beaten the Raptors fourteen straight times, going back to December of twenty twenty-three. Fourteen in a row against one team. That’s dominance. And it’s not because Toronto has been bad the whole time. The Raptors have had good players and good stretches in that span. The Knicks have just found a way, every single time, including on a night when they were down thirteen in the fourth quarter.

Now let’s talk about the player who kept the Knicks alive long enough for Brunson’s shot: Landry Shamet.

Shamet came off the bench and scored twenty-three points in thirty minutes. He went eight of twelve from the field, with four threes. He opened overtime with a three that put the Knicks in front. He converted a huge and-one. And he scored seven of his twenty-three in the extra period. That was a big bounce-back after he scored just five in the loss to Indiana.

But here’s the thing. This wasn’t a one-night story. Shamet has been one of the Knicks’ best players all season. Through thirteen games, the video says he’s shooting about forty-two percent from three. And on defense, opponents are shooting only about twenty-nine point seven percent from three when he’s the primary defender. That’s elite, and it’s not a small sample anymore.

The lineup data is even more impressive. According to Daily Knicks, four of the Knicks’ five most-used lineups, ranked by net rating, include Shamet. With him on the floor, the Knicks are allowing about one hundred five points per one hundred possessions, which is in the ninety-fifth percentile. Opponents have an effective field goal percentage of about forty-seven percent, in the ninety-seventh percentile. The Knicks’ point differential with Shamet on the court is about plus sixteen per one hundred possessions. And among Knicks players with more than one hundred fifty minutes, Shamet ranks first on the team in on-off net rating, at about plus fourteen.

In plain English: the Knicks have been way better with Landry Shamet on the floor than without him. And the gap is bigger for him than for anyone else on the roster. That’s not what most people expected from a guy who signed for about twenty-four million dollars over four years this summer. That deal got almost no attention. Thirteen games in, it looks like one of the smartest moves Leon Rose has made.

And Shamet’s story makes this even better. He’s bounced around the league. Philadelphia, the Clippers, Brooklyn, Phoenix, Washington. He came to the Knicks without much fanfare, and he had to fight through a shoulder injury early in his time in New York. Teams have always known he could shoot. What nobody expected was that he’d turn into one of the most reliable two-way role players on a championship team. That’s a credit to him, and it’s a credit to a coaching staff that found the right role for him.

What makes Shamet so valuable is how he fits next to the stars. Brunson and Towns draw so much attention that defenses have to send help. When they do, the Knicks need someone who punishes them. That’s Shamet. He moves without the ball, he’s always ready to shoot, and he keeps the floor spaced so Towns can work inside and Brunson can attack. And now, he’s adding real defense too. He’s quick on his rotations, he competes on the perimeter, and he makes it hard for guards to get clean looks. For a team that built its title run on defense, a bench player who helps on both ends is exactly what Brown needs.

And remember what Brown said after the second Cleveland game. He said it’s going to be hard to find minutes for Mikal Bridges at times, and even for Josh Hart, because Shamet and Miles McBride are playing at such a high level. Sunday showed exactly why. Shamet played thirty minutes and was one of the main reasons the Knicks won.

That leads to a fair question that the original video starts to get into: should Shamet start? He started three games earlier this month while Hart was out, against Chicago, Cleveland and Milwaukee, and the Knicks won all three. Now that Hart is back, he’s coming off the bench again. Here’s my view. I’d keep him in a sixth-man role, but with starter minutes. That’s what Brown is doing now, and it works. The starting group with Hart is built around toughness and rebounding. Shamet gives you a jolt off the bench, and he can close games. Who finishes matters more than who starts, especially for a team that’s been losing close games. And on Sunday, Shamet finished.

Let me also say something about the contract, because it matters for the bigger picture. Shamet is on one of the most team-friendly deals on the roster. He can’t be traded until January fifteenth because of when he re-signed. But even after that date, I can’t imagine the Knicks wanting to move him. With Towns’ contract question, Hart’s team option, Bridges’ rising salary and McBride’s expiring deal, the Knicks need cheap players who can actually play. Shamet is exactly that. He’s the kind of signing that keeps a contender under the second apron without giving up talent.

There’s one more thing about Brunson’s minutes that’s worth watching. Forty-one minutes is a lot for a player coming off wrist surgery this summer, in a regular-season game in November. It made sense on Sunday, because the Knicks needed the win. But Brown has to be careful. Brunson has already played a lot of heavy minutes in close games this season. The whole point of having a deep bench with Shamet, McBride, Kolek and Clarkson is so Brunson doesn’t have to play forty minutes every night. The more the bench can build leads, the less Brunson has to rescue the team at the end.

And let’s be clear about what Shamet’s emergence means for the rest of the rotation. Every minute Shamet earns is a minute someone else doesn’t get. Right now, that’s mostly coming out of Mikal Bridges’ playing time, and some nights out of Hart’s. That’s not a punishment for anyone. It’s just Brown playing the guys who are playing best, exactly like he said he would. And if Bridges keeps building on his eighteen-point game against Indiana, the competition for those minutes gets even more interesting. That’s the healthiest kind of problem a coach can have.

So where does all this leave the Knicks? Let me give you three takeaways.

Takeaway number one: the closing ability isn’t gone. One win doesn’t fix a one and six clutch record. But the way they did it matters. Down thirteen in the fourth on the road, bad end to regulation, and they still won. That’s the old Knicks, and it’s the version of this team that won a championship in June.

Takeaway number two: Brunson can’t do it alone every night. He played forty-one minutes and took twenty-two shots. That’s too much for a team trying to stay fresh for April. The Knicks need more help in the fourth quarter, and they got it from Shamet and Towns on Sunday. They need that every night.

Takeaway number three: Shamet is the real deal. Forty-two percent from three, elite defense on shooters, and the best on-off numbers on the team. That’s not a hot streak. That’s a player who’s become a core part of the rotation.

Let me also put seven and six in context. It’s not where anyone expected the defending champions to be in the middle of November. But it’s also a lot better than where they were a few weeks ago at oh and three. Since then, they’ve gone seven and three. And most of their losses have been close games, decided in the last few possessions. That’s why a win like Sunday’s matters so much. If the Knicks can turn even a couple of those close losses into wins over the next month, the record will start to look like a contender again. The East is crowded, with Boston, Philadelphia, Detroit, Cleveland and others all in the mix. There’s plenty of time to climb. But the climb starts with winning games like this one.

And if you’re looking for the double dose of good news the title promised, here it is. Dose one: the Knicks proved they can still close. Dose two: Landry Shamet has turned into one of the most valuable role players in the league. That’s the real double dose. No fake trade required, and no fake insider either.

And one more thing about the title. A two hundred million dollar blockbuster incoming. The only two hundred million dollar number is still the four-year extension offer Towns turned down this summer, and that’s a story for next summer, when Towns decides on his sixty-one million dollar player option. There’s no blockbuster trade coming. The best news this week came from the floor, not from a fake report.

A more honest title would be something like, the Knicks finally close one: Brunson’s overtime dagger in Toronto. Or, Landry Shamet is the Knicks’ secret weapon, and the numbers prove it.
