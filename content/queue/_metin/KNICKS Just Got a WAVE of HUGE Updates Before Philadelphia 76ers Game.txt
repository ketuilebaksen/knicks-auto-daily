The Knicks spent last week trying to move Kolek or Pacôme Dadiet to make room for Bruce Brown. Nobody bit. On Saturday, the Knicks waived Bruce Brown instead. Kolek stays.

Update number one, the roster.

On Saturday, the Knicks waived Bruce Brown after failing to find a trade partner. Stefan Bondy of the Post reported that no deal could be found, even though the Knicks were willing to attach second-round picks, and they have eleven of those over the next seven drafts. The problem, as we explained last time, was the market: a receiving team needed cap room or a trade exception plus an open roster spot, on the weekend when every team in the league was cutting players.

James Wiseman is expected to take the fourteenth and final roster spot. Over four preseason games, he had thirty-two points, seventeen rebounds and three blocks in forty-six minutes. He'll be the third center behind Karl-Anthony Towns and Andre Drummond. Mike Brown described him this week as "young, he's talented, he's raw, still."

And there was one more move. The Knicks signed Dink Pate to an Exhibit 10 deal and waived him so he can return to Westchester, after he left Providence College when the NCAA never cleared his eligibility. He's twenty, six-eight, and he averaged about sixteen points and almost seven rebounds in Westchester last season. We broke that move down in detail in the last video.

So here's the roster for Tuesday. Fourteen standard contracts: Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, Karl-Anthony Towns and Josh Hart as the starters, then Deuce McBride, Jose Alvarado, Landry Shamet, Jordan Clarkson, Tyler Kolek, Mohamed Diawara, Pacôme Dadiet, Andre Drummond and James Wiseman. Plus the two-way players, Kevin McCullar, Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins. No fifteenth man, and the Knicks sit just under the second apron.

Before we move on, a quick word on what that roster means for the rest of the season. Because the Knicks are above the first apron, there are rules that follow them all year. They can't take back more salary than they send out in a trade. And in the buyout market later in the season, they can't sign a player who was waived if his salary before the buyout was above the mid-level exception. So the usual contender trick, picking up a veteran who gets bought out in February, is mostly closed to them, except for players who were already on smaller deals. That's another reason the front office was so careful with these last two roster spots. It may be a while before they get another chance to add someone.

And let me put the preseason in one place, because the record will come up on Tuesday's broadcast. The Knicks lost in Philadelphia, a hundred and twenty to ninety-seven, with the starters playing one half. They lost to Washington at the Garden, a hundred and eleven to a hundred and nine. They lost to Minnesota, a hundred and twenty-two to a hundred and four. They lost in Toronto, a hundred and forty-eight to ninety-eight, with all five starters and three key reserves resting. And then they won the finale against Toronto, a hundred and twenty-two to a hundred and twenty, with Towns one assist short of a triple-double. One and four. If you've followed along, you know what mattered: the starters looked sharp when they played, and the bench defense was a problem all month. That's the storyline to carry into Tuesday, not the record.

Update number two, Tyler Kolek. What happens now?

This is a strange spot for a player. For a week, his name was in trade talks. One Post report even said Kolek has little value around the league. And now he's still a Knick, and he'll walk into the locker room on Tuesday knowing the front office was willing to move him.

So let's look at what he actually is, because the trade-market verdict and the basketball verdict aren't the same thing. Last season, his second, Kolek played sixty-two games, about twelve minutes a night, averaging four point four points, one point six rebounds and two point seven assists. He shot about forty-four percent from the field and almost thirty-nine percent from three. He played eight playoff games in a smaller role. And he had one of the biggest nights of the Knicks' season in the NBA Cup final, with fourteen points, five rebounds and five assists. On a cheap contract, about two point three million this season with a team option for next season at about two and a half, that's a useful player.

So why did the market say no? Partly the timing. Teams with cap room this weekend could pick from a lot of options, and many of them were looking for size or specific roles. Partly his position. Backup point guards on minimum-level deals are not rare. And partly his preseason, where he shot about thirty-six percent. That doesn't mean he can't play. It means nobody was willing to give the Knicks value for him on cutdown weekend.

One of the comments on a fan channel this week compared him to T.J. McConnell, the Pacers' backup point guard. I understand the comparison. Like McConnell, Kolek is a pass-first organizer with a high motor who doesn't need the ball to score. But let's be careful. McConnell built that reputation over years of productive minutes and big playoff moments. Kolek isn't there yet. The best version of Kolek this season is a reliable fifth guard who keeps the offense organized when Brunson sits, takes care of the ball, and hits open threes.

And there's an interesting idea worth thinking about: Kolek and Alvarado on the floor together. Alvarado is a pest on defense and a cutter on offense. Kolek moves the ball and moves well without it. A bench backcourt like that could spread the floor for Shamet and Clarkson. Will Mike Brown try it? Maybe in spots. But with McBride, Alvarado, Shamet and Clarkson all ahead of him most nights, Kolek is going to have stretches of DNPs. The best thing he can do is what Mike Brown has been saying since day one. Every day is a competition. If Kolek practices the right way, his minutes will come when there's an injury.

Let me make the honest case for the market's view too, because I've been defending Kolek. He's six-two, he's not an elite athlete, and his defense, while improved, can be targeted by bigger guards. In the playoffs last season, he played only eight games and fewer than seven minutes a night. When it matters most, Mike Brown has trusted McBride, Alvarado and Shamet ahead of him. So when a rival executive looks at Kolek, he sees a nice regular-season backup who hasn't shown he can hold up in May. That's why the trade value is low. It's not that he isn't a player. It's that he hasn't yet shown he's a playoff player.

And if the Knicks revisit a trade near the February deadline, Kolek's cheap contract and team option will still be useful. That's probably the realistic future. Not gone this week. Possibly part of a bigger deal later.

Update number three, Josh Hart's ankle.

Hart missed Thursday's preseason finale against Toronto with soreness in his right ankle. That's worth flagging, because Hart is the glue of this team and he's already playing through the ring-finger injury we've talked about many times, the one where the only remaining fix would be a joint replacement that would end his career. The good news: according to Ian Begley, Mike Brown doesn't think the ankle will affect Hart's status for Tuesday. So the expectation is that Hart plays. But it's the kind of thing you check right before tip-off.

And there was a related detail from the finale. With Hart out, Mike Brown started Mohamed Diawara, specifically so Diawara could match up with Kawhi Leonard and Scottie Barnes. That's a big vote of confidence in a young defender. Which brings us to the next update.

Update number four, Mohamed Diawara.

Diawara is twenty-one, and this summer the Knicks re-signed him for four years and about eleven point two million dollars. That's a team-friendly contract for a young wing, and the reason the Knicks were so careful with their bench spending was to keep exactly that kind of cheap, developing player. Mike Brown talked about him earlier this month. He said Diawara looks more comfortable this season, moves more freely on offense, communicates better and understands that he's supposed to direct teammates on defense. And then he said what he wants: Diawara needs to take the next step.

That's important for two reasons. First, the Knicks just waived Bruce Brown, a veteran defender. A lot of the minutes Brown might have played on the wing will go to Diawara instead. Second, the Knicks' bench defense was the biggest weakness of the preseason. If Diawara takes that next step, he's part of the answer. If he doesn't, the front office's decision to keep youth over experience will get a lot of criticism.

Update number five, Andre Drummond.

Here's something that didn't get much attention. After the Minnesota game, Mike Brown went out of his way to praise Drummond. "His communication right now is unbelievable," he said. He mentioned the blocks, defending the pick-and-roll the right way, and being big. And then: "But just watching him, and more importantly listening to him, man, that got me really excited." Drummond finished the preseason strong too, with fourteen points on six of seven shooting, eight rebounds and four assists in the finale. When this channel started covering the backup-center question back in September, the worry was that the Knicks hadn't replaced Mitchell Robinson. Drummond isn't Robinson. But on the evidence of the preseason, he's going to be a very good backup, and his voice on defense is something the bench needs.

Update number six, Karl-Anthony Towns and the clock.

Here's the timing that matters. Once the regular season starts, a veteran can only sign an extension if his contract is in its final year, and option years count. Towns has this season plus his player option for 2027-28. So at tip-off on Tuesday, the window for an in-season extension closes. The Knicks paused all extension talks on October 9th, and nothing has changed since. So unless something surprising happens in the next forty-eight hours, Towns will play this season without an extension, and the next chance will be next summer.

What's the outlook? ESPN's Brian Windhorst said he expects the Knicks and Towns to eventually come to an agreement, despite the paused talks, and that several teams would pursue Towns if he ever reached free agency. That fits what we covered last time. An anonymous executive told the Post that Chicago and the Clippers have the cleanest path to cap space next summer, but that even the best-positioned teams could probably offer about forty-five to fifty million a year, which is below what the Knicks can pay. So the story isn't over. It's just paused until 2027.

And Brunson? He had surgery on his left wrist this summer, the one he played through during the title run. He's looked normal in the preseason, and he scored twenty in the finale. He said about playing through it, "I just knew that the opportunity rarely presents itself, you know, to be in a position like that." That's the captain.

Update number seven, the opponent. And this is the one that should get your attention.

The Sixers coming into the Garden aren't the team the Knicks swept in the second round. Two huge moves happened this summer. First, LeBron James is now in Philadelphia. He's starting a record twenty-fourth NBA season, with his fourth franchise. Second, and I want to make sure everyone knows this, on July 1st, Shams reported that Boston traded Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia for Paul George, two first-round picks and two second-round picks. So Jaylen Brown makes his Sixers debut on Tuesday, at the Garden.

So think about that lineup: LeBron, Jaylen Brown, Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey and the young guard VJ Edgecombe, with Nick Nurse coaching. That's a lot of talent, and it was put together specifically to beat the Knicks. LeBron said at media day he "didn't leave my family to lose in the second round." And by the way, if you've seen fake trade videos this month saying Karl-Anthony Towns could join Jaylen Brown in Boston, that's how you know they're out of date. Jaylen Brown isn't in Boston anymore.

It's worth thinking about what that trade did to the East as a whole. Jaylen Brown is a former Finals MVP, from Boston's 2024 title. Philadelphia gave up Paul George, who's thirty-six, plus four draft picks to get him, which tells you how badly the Sixers wanted a second elite wing next to LeBron. And Boston, which now has Paul George and also Mitchell Robinson, the Knicks' former center, is retooling around Jayson Tatum. Miami has Giannis. Toronto has Kawhi. Washington has Anthony Davis, Trae Young and the number-one pick. The East didn't just get better at the top. It got deeper. One sportsbook has the Knicks as the narrow favorite to win the conference, at about a twenty-two percent implied chance, just ahead of Philadelphia at about twenty. That's the market saying: you're the champions, but barely the favorites.

The Sixers do have one gap on their bench. Ariel Hukporti, the former Knick who signed with Philadelphia, is out for the season. That's a tough loss for a player who was part of a title team.

And the history between these teams is real. They split the regular-season series last year. Then the Knicks swept Philadelphia in the second round. That sweep is a big reason the Sixers rebuilt their roster the way they did. So Tuesday isn't just ring night. It's the first test against the team that reshaped itself to beat you.

Now, how should you read Tuesday's result? Carefully. It's one game. Both teams will be rusty. Philadelphia is still learning how to play with three new stars. And the Knicks have the emotion of the ring ceremony, which can help or hurt. Sportsbooks have the Knicks as roughly a two-to-one favorite at home. That sounds right to me. But I wouldn't overreact to a win or a loss. What matters is what it tells us about the questions we've been tracking.

And here's the logistics for anyone planning to watch. The game is on NBC, at seven p.m. Eastern, the middle game of an opening-night tripleheader. Boston opens at Detroit in the afternoon, and Oklahoma City plays at San Antonio in the late game. That's the team the Knicks beat in the Finals, by the way, so Tuesday night will feature both of last season's finalists. Victor Wembanyama told reporters at media day that the loss was haunting. Expect a lot of Finals talk on the broadcast.

So here's what I'll be watching on Tuesday night.

One, the start after the ceremony. The Knicks raise their first championship banner since 1973 and hand out the rings before tip-off. That's an emotional half hour. Watch the first six minutes. If the Knicks come out flat, Philadelphia has enough talent to punish them.

Two, the matchups on the wing. Who guards Jaylen Brown? Who guards LeBron? Anunoby and Bridges will take most of that, and Hart if his ankle's fine. If Diawara gets minutes, watch whether Mike Brown trusts him on one of those stars, the way he did with Kawhi on Thursday.

Three, the bench defense. This was the biggest weakness of the preseason. Thirty-nine fast-break points in Philadelphia in the opener. Eighteen threes from Minnesota. Thirty fast-break points in a half in Toronto. Against a team with LeBron and Maxey pushing the pace, transition defense will be tested from the first minute.

Four, the center rotation. Embiid is still one of the most physical big men in the league. Watch how Towns handles him, whether Drummond's physicality matters in his minutes, and whether Wiseman gets in at all.

Five, Towns at the four. Mike Brown hinted this month that Towns might play a little more power forward. Against a big Philadelphia lineup, that could be the night to try it, with Drummond at center.

And six, Brunson's shooting. The wrist has looked fine. A good shooting night on the biggest stage would put that question to rest for good.

So that's the wave of updates. No trade. Bruce Brown waived. Wiseman in. Dink Pate back in Westchester. Kolek stays. Hart's ankle should be fine. Diawara and Drummond have earned the coach's trust. The Towns extension window closes at tip-off. And a loaded Sixers team, with LeBron and Jaylen Brown, walks into the Garden for banner night.
