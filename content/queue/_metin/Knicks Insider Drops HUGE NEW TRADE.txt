Knicks insider just dropped a huge new trade. Let’s be clear. There’s no new trade. Nobody has reported a deal, a target, or even serious talks. What actually happened is that SNY’s Ian Begley said the Knicks should be evaluating their backup center options, and that front offices study dozens of trade scenarios all year long. That’s true, and it’s useful context. But it’s not a trade. The original video takes that and runs with it, saying the Knicks are preparing to make a trade, and that Miles McBride is likely gone. So today, let’s separate what Begley actually said from what the video added, and then let’s look at the one piece of this story that really is new and interesting: James Wiseman’s contract, and why the window between December fifteenth and January tenth could matter.

Let’s start with Begley, because his words are the real news here. He said, you have to be evaluating your backup options. Even if you feel comfortable with Andre Drummond, you have to see what’s out there. And then he gave some background on the summer. According to the video, Begley said the Knicks checked in with Dallas, made an offer sheet to a young center there, talked to Charlotte about Moussa Diabaté, and were also in touch with New Orleans. In other words, they were in touch all over the league looking for a backup big. I’d treat the exact details of those summer talks as the video’s version of Begley’s comments. But the overall picture matches what we already knew. Stefan Bondy of the New York Post wrote that the Knicks searched for backup bigs all summer and came up empty.

So Begley’s point was simple. Losing Mitchell Robinson left a hole. Drummond has done a good job, but every front office is constantly studying the market. If the Knicks can find the right backup center at the right price, they should look at it. That’s not a trade update. That’s just how front offices work. Begley even said it directly: front offices and scouts work internally on dozens of different free agent and trade scenarios through the year.

Now, the video takes it a step further. It says, in its words, that the Knicks are preparing to make a trade at the deadline, and that this proves it. I’d be careful with that. Studying the market is not the same as preparing a deal. Every contender studies the market. Some of them make moves. Many of them don’t. Begley didn’t say a trade is coming. He said the Knicks should be looking. Those are very different things.

Then the video brings in Michael Scotto, who wrote about how Landry Shamet’s play could allow the Knicks to use Miles McBride as a trade chip in the final year of his contract. That makes sense. Shamet has been the Knicks’ best bench player. He has the best on-off net rating on the team. He just scored twenty-three in the overtime win in Toronto and made the game-saving steal in Minnesota. When one guard is playing that well, another guard becomes more expendable. And right now, McBride’s minutes are falling. He played fourteen and nine minutes in the two Toronto games and just eight against Minnesota. Sports Illustrated even said Tyler Kolek may have passed him in the rotation.

So yes, McBride is the most logical trade piece on the roster. He’s on an expiring contract, he’s a free agent next summer, and the Knicks may not be able to afford to keep him without crossing the second apron. But the video goes a little too far when it says McBride is likely going to be traded, as if it’s decided. Nobody has reported that. And there’s a real argument for keeping him. Daily Knicks pointed out that last season, the Knicks kept Mitchell Robinson on an expiring deal through the deadline, and he helped them win a championship before leaving in free agency. That’s not a mistake if it gets you a ring.

Now let’s talk about the money, because the video gets this part right. McBride makes about four million dollars. The Knicks are above the first apron, so they can’t take back more salary than they send out. And there aren’t many useful backup centers making four million or less. So if the Knicks trade McBride for a center, they’ll almost certainly need to add a second player to match a bigger salary. That’s the key question. Who’s the second player?

The video has an interesting answer: James Wiseman. And this is the part of the story that’s actually new.

According to the video, Wiseman is on a non-guaranteed contract. His deal doesn’t become fully guaranteed until January tenth, and he becomes trade-eligible on December fifteenth. If that’s right, it creates a small but important window. Here’s why.

When a team trades a player whose contract isn’t guaranteed yet, his full salary still counts for matching purposes in the trade. But the team that receives him can waive him before the guarantee date and avoid paying most of that money. That makes a non-guaranteed contract very attractive to teams that want to save money. It’s like a coupon. It helps the Knicks match salaries, and it costs the other team almost nothing if they don’t want to keep the player.

So between December fifteenth, when Wiseman becomes trade-eligible, and January tenth, when his contract becomes guaranteed, he could be one of the most useful trade pieces the Knicks have. Not because of what he does on the floor, but because of what his contract does in a trade. That’s a smart detail, and it’s the kind of thing front offices think about all the time.

Let’s do the math. McBride makes about three point nine six million. If Wiseman is on a veteran minimum deal of about two point four five million, the two of them together come to about six point four million. In a two-for-one trade, the Knicks would drop to thirteen players and need to add one more within two weeks. So the incoming center would probably have to make somewhere between about four and three-quarter million and six million dollars, depending on how the Knicks fill the last roster spot. That’s a real backup big. Not a star, but someone who could protect the rim when Karl-Anthony Towns sits.

There’s a timing catch, though. ESPN’s Bobby Marks noted that the Knicks can’t sign a new player until around February fourteenth, because they’re only about eight hundred nine thousand dollars under the second apron, and a prorated minimum contract only fits under that cushion late in the season. So a two-for-one trade in December or early January would leave the Knicks short a player for more than two weeks, which isn’t allowed. That’s a problem. The video suggests one possible solution at the end: converting a two-way player to a standard contract. That’s a real option. A two-way player like Dalton Knecht could be converted, but his new standard salary would still count against the cap and the apron, so the numbers would have to fit. It’s possible, but it isn’t simple.

So here’s the honest version. The Wiseman window is interesting, but it’s tight. A two-for-one deal using Wiseman’s non-guaranteed contract makes the most financial sense before January tenth, but the roster rules make a two-for-one easier closer to the February deadline. The Knicks would have to find a way to make both work, maybe by doing a one-for-one, or by finding a team willing to send back two players. That’s the kind of puzzle front offices solve all the time, but it’s not as easy as the video makes it sound.

The video also mentions other combinations: McBride and Pacôme Dadiet, or McBride and Tyler Kolek. McBride and Dadiet together come to about six point nine four million, a little more than the Wiseman version. Dadiet is also on an expiring contract, after the Knicks declined his option in October. That’s a clean option. And after December fifteenth, McBride and Jose Alvarado together would come to about eight point four million, which opens up even more possibilities, including a player like Orlando’s Goga Bitadze, who makes about seven point six million on an expiring deal.

As for Kolek, the video says he’s firmly the backup point guard and the Knicks shouldn’t trade him. I mostly agree. Kolek has been excellent. He had twenty-five assists and only two turnovers in fifty-seven minutes while Alvarado was out. He scored twelve on five of six shooting against Milwaukee. He’s cheap, he’s young, and he’s under team control next season. Marc Stein reported in October that the Knicks would only move Kolek for a coveted center. And Begley has said Kolek could be part of a deal if Drummond isn’t the answer in April. So Kolek isn’t untouchable. But he’d have to be part of a deal for a real difference-maker, not just any backup big.

Now let’s talk about who the target could be. The video mentions the teams the Knicks talked to in the summer, New Orleans and Charlotte, and adds Milwaukee, suggesting Kel’el Ware. The video’s logic is that the Bucks are struggling now, so everything is on the table. I’d be careful there. Ware is young, cheap and talented, and Milwaukee got him as part of the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade. A rebuilding team usually wants to keep players like that, not trade them for an expiring guard. So he’d be expensive. The more realistic names are the ones that have come up in columns: Charles Bassey of the Warriors, whose cap number matches Drummond’s to the dollar; Goga Bitadze of the Magic, who’s on an expiring deal and stuck behind two other centers; and young bigs on cheap deals like Yanic Konan Niederhäuser or Baba Miller of the Clippers. None of those have been reported as Knicks targets. But they’re the kind of players who fit the money.

And let’s be fair to Drummond. He’s been better lately. Mike Brown named him the defensive player of the game in the first Cleveland win, when he held Evan Mobley to oh for three. He had fourteen rebounds in sixteen minutes against Milwaukee. The Knicks just held Minnesota to six offensive rebounds. If Drummond keeps playing like that, the urgency to make a trade goes down. Begley’s line was that Drummond has done a great job, but the Knicks still have to look. That’s the right balance.

Let me also give the video credit for one more point. It says Wiseman was signed as an emergency option, in case Towns got hurt or Drummond couldn’t play, and that he hasn’t been part of the rotation. That’s fair. Wiseman is only twenty-five. He was the second pick in the twenty twenty draft, and he’s had a brutal run of injuries, including a torn Achilles in October of twenty twenty-four. The Knicks gave him a chance, and he had some good moments in the preseason. But he hasn’t earned real minutes yet. If he becomes a trade piece because of his contract, that’s not a knock on him. It’s just how roster building works.

Let me lay out the calendar, because the dates are what make this story interesting.

December fifteenth is the first big date. That’s when most of the players the Knicks signed in the offseason become trade-eligible: Jose Alvarado, Jordan Clarkson, Mohamed Diawara, Andre Drummond and, according to the video, James Wiseman. It’s also the date when Charles Bassey becomes trade-eligible for Golden State. So before December fifteenth, a lot of the combinations people talk about can’t happen. After it, the menu gets much bigger.

January tenth is the second date, if the video is right about Wiseman’s contract. Before that day, a team that receives Wiseman can waive him and avoid most of his salary. After that day, his contract is guaranteed for the season, and he becomes a normal minimum salary in any trade. Still useful for matching, but no longer a coupon.

January fifteenth is the third date. That’s when Landry Shamet becomes trade-eligible. I don’t expect the Knicks to trade Shamet, the way he’s playing. But it matters because some proposals out there, like the Goga Bitadze idea with Shamet, Wiseman and two second-round picks, simply can’t happen before that day.

February eleventh is the trade deadline. And February fourteenth is roughly when the Knicks can finally fit a prorated minimum contract under the second apron. Put those two dates side by side and you see the problem. If the Knicks make a two-for-one trade on deadline day, they’d drop to thirteen players, sign someone around three days later, and stay inside the two-week limit. That works. If they make the same trade on December twentieth, they’d be short a player for almost two months. That doesn’t work, unless they convert a two-way contract or bring back two players.

So the Wiseman window and the roster window point in opposite directions. One says go early. The other says go late. That’s why I don’t think the video’s version, where a deal is basically ready, holds up. The more likely path, if the Knicks do anything, is a one-for-one, or a two-for-two, or a move closer to February.

Now let’s talk about why McBride and Dadiet might be the cleanest combo of all. Both contracts are expiring. Neither player is in the regular rotation right now. Together they send out about six point nine four million, which is more than the Wiseman version, so the Knicks could take back a slightly bigger salary. And there’s no guarantee date to worry about, so the deal could happen any time before the deadline. The cost is that Dadiet is a young player with real tools, and the Knicks used a first-round pick on him. Trading him means giving up on that development. But if the return is a reliable backup center for a title run, a lot of fans would make that trade.

And let’s not forget what McBride means to this team. He’s a homegrown Knick, drafted in twenty twenty-one, and he’s been one of the best point-of-attack defenders on the roster for years. This is his contract year, and the minutes have dried up just as he needs to show what he can do. That’s tough for any player. If the Knicks do trade him, it might even be good for him, a chance to play real minutes somewhere else before free agency. If they keep him, he’s insurance. If Brunson or Shamet goes down, McBride is the guy who steps in. Contenders need that kind of depth in April and May.

Let’s also remember the context. The Knicks are nine and six. They started oh and three, and since then they’ve been one of the better teams in the East. They beat Toronto in overtime, beat them again, and then won a one-possession game in Minnesota. Towns is putting up double-doubles every night, chasing Willis Reed’s franchise record. Brunson is closing games again. This isn’t a team in crisis. It’s a team with one clear need, and that need has been the same since July. That’s exactly the kind of team that studies the market calmly, instead of panicking.

So let’s put it all together.

Is there a new trade? No. Begley said the Knicks should be evaluating backup centers, which every smart front office does.

Are the Knicks looking for a backup center? Yes. That’s been clear since the summer, and Begley, Bondy and ESPN all agree it’s the biggest need.

Is McBride likely to be traded? He’s the most logical piece, because of his expiring contract and his falling minutes. But nothing has been reported, and there’s a real case for keeping him for a title run.

What’s actually new? The Wiseman contract detail. If his deal isn’t guaranteed until January tenth, he could be a useful salary-matching piece between December fifteenth and January tenth, because another team could take him on and waive him without paying much.

What’s the catch? The Knicks’ roster and apron rules make two-for-one trades much easier close to the February deadline than in December or January. So any deal would need careful timing.

What should fans watch for? Watch McBride’s minutes over the next few weeks. Watch whether Wiseman gets any garbage-time run before January tenth. And watch Drummond against the best rebounding teams. Those three things will tell you more than any clickbait title.

Teams don’t give away good young centers for nothing, and the Knicks didn’t have much to offer under the apron rules. So they signed Drummond and Wiseman, two bigs on minimum deals, and decided to evaluate during the season. That’s what’s happening now. Begley’s comments aren’t a sign that something is about to break. They’re a sign that the plan from July is still the plan.
