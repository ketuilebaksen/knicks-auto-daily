The title says Leon Rose did it again, and that a massive trade has been confirmed. Let’s be clear right away. There’s no trade. Nothing was confirmed except one transaction, and it’s not even an NBA contract. Here’s what actually happened. Rob Dillingham, the former number eight pick in the twenty twenty-four draft, signed with the Westchester Knicks, the Knicks’ G League team. That was reported by Jake Fischer and The Stein Line, and according to The Stein Line, he signed on Wednesday. That’s the whole story. It’s an interesting story, and it’s worth talking about. But it’s a G League signing, not a trade, and definitely not a massive one. So let’s do the honest version. Who Dillingham is, why his career stalled, what a G League deal actually means, whether he could ever play for the Knicks, and where the original video goes too far.

Let’s start with who he is, because a lot of fans may only know the name. Rob Dillingham was a one-and-done guard at Kentucky. He was known as one of the most dangerous scorers in college basketball that year. Shifty, quick, a great ball handler, and a guy who could just get buckets. The Minnesota Timberwolves took him eighth overall in twenty twenty-four. That’s a lottery pick. Teams don’t take players that high by accident. Scouts saw a player who could create his own shot against anybody, and who had the kind of quickness you can’t teach. That part of the scouting report hasn’t changed.

But the NBA has been a lot harder for him. As a rookie in Minnesota, he barely played. According to the video, he averaged about four and a half points and two assists in about ten and a half minutes a game over forty-nine games. Wolves fans kept asking Chris Finch to give him a longer leash, and it never really happened. Then in February, halfway through his second season, Minnesota traded him to Chicago, along with Leonard Miller and four second-round picks, for Ayo Dosunmu and Julian Phillips.

In Chicago, he finally got some minutes. In thirty games with the Bulls, the video says he averaged nine point six points, three rebounds and two point eight assists in about twenty-one and a half minutes. That’s more like it. But over his first two seasons combined, he shot about forty-one percent from the field and about thirty-two and a half percent from three. For a player whose whole calling card is shot making, those numbers aren’t good enough. And then the Bulls traded him to the Charlotte Hornets for Buddy Hield. Now, a couple of years after being a top-ten pick, he’s in the G League. That’s a hard fall. And it says a lot about how quickly things can change in this league. Three teams in less than a year, and now a G League contract. But it also means he’s still only at the start of his story. Plenty of players have bounced around early in their careers and then found the right fit. The question is whether he can be one of them.

One correction: the video calls him twenty years old. He’s twenty-one. He was born in January of twenty oh five. Still very young, which is actually part of why this is interesting.

So why hasn’t it worked? Mostly size and strength. Dillingham is listed around six-two and about one hundred seventy-five pounds. In the NBA, that makes defense very hard. Opponents target small guards on switches, and they try to bully them in the post. On offense, the shots he made in college are harder to get against longer, stronger defenders. The talent is still there. The question is whether he can turn it into winning basketball at this level.

Now, the most important part: what does a G League deal actually mean? This is where the original video gets it wrong.

A G League contract is not an NBA contract. Dillingham is not on the Knicks’ roster. He doesn’t count against the salary cap. He can’t play for the Knicks in an NBA game unless the Knicks sign him to an NBA deal, either a standard contract or a two-way contract. And here’s the big one: while he’s in the G League, the Knicks don’t have exclusive rights to him. If another NBA team likes what it sees, it can sign him. That’s how the G League works. It’s a place to get playing time and get noticed by all thirty teams, not just one.

The video says Leon Rose probably worked out some under-the-table deals to make sure these players stay with New York. Let me be really clear about this. There’s no evidence of that, and it’s not a good thing to joke about. Under-the-table agreements to get around league rules are exactly what the NBA punishes. Remember, the Knicks already lost a second-round pick a couple of years ago after the league found they’d made early contact with Jalen Brunson before free agency. They’re not going to risk that kind of trouble over a G League guard. So no, this isn’t some secret master plan. It’s a normal G League signing. The Knicks are giving a talented young player a place to play, and in return, they get a close look at him.

Now, could he end up on the Knicks? Let’s look at the roster. The Knicks have fourteen standard contracts, and they’re only about eight hundred twenty-four thousand dollars under the second apron. They also have three two-way contracts, and all three are full. When the Knicks signed Dalton Knecht to a two-way deal in October, they waived Kevin McCullar Jr. to make room, and Knecht joined the two other two-way players. So if the Knicks wanted to give Dillingham a two-way contract this season, they’d have to waive one of those players first. That’s possible, but there’s no sign it’s coming.

That’s why the video is actually right about one thing: this move is more about the future than about this season. Daily Knicks wrote that Dillingham could compete for backup minutes next season if he shows real improvement. That’s a fair way to put it. Not now. Maybe later. If he plays well in Westchester.

And the video makes another fair point. The Knicks’ back court is completely stacked. Jalen Brunson, Josh Hart, Landry Shamet, Miles McBride, Tyler Kolek, Jose Alvarado and Jordan Clarkson. That’s seven guards fighting for minutes. Kolek just scored twelve points on five of six shooting against Milwaukee, and he’s battling Alvarado for the backup point guard job. There’s no room for another small guard in the NBA rotation right now. So Dillingham’s job in Westchester is simple: play a lot, get better, and make himself impossible to ignore.

Let me also fix a detail about Dalton Knecht. The video says Knecht was a first-round pick of the Lakers in the same draft as Dillingham. That’s right. Knecht went seventeenth in twenty twenty-four. The Lakers waived him during the preseason, and the Knicks signed him to a two-way contract. He spent time in Westchester, and then he scored ten points in his Knicks debut against Chicago. So there’s a real example of how this path can work. Knecht is the model. Dillingham is the next one to try it.

And Knecht’s story is worth a closer look, because it shows how fast a player’s value can change. As a rookie with the Lakers, Knecht averaged about nine points and shot about thirty-seven and a half percent from three. He even had a game where he hit nine threes and scored thirty-seven points against Utah, tying a rookie record. Then last season, his numbers dropped to about four points a game, and in October, the Lakers waived him and ate more than four million dollars of guaranteed salary. A year and a half after that thirty-seven-point night, he was available for nothing. The Knicks grabbed him on a two-way deal, and now he’s already shown he can help. That’s exactly the kind of bet Dillingham represents.

There’s also a third name the video mentions: Dink Pate. He’s another young player in Westchester. Pate is a fun story of his own. He’s only twenty, born in March of twenty oh six, and he’s six-eight. He played in Westchester last season and averaged about sixteen points, almost seven rebounds and more than three and a half assists. Then he tried to go to college at Providence, but the NCAA never cleared him to play. So in October, he signed an Exhibit Ten deal with the Knicks and was waived back to Westchester. That’s a real prospect. So the Knicks are building a little pipeline of young talent in the G League. That’s smart. Under the new collective bargaining agreement, cheap, young, developing players are incredibly valuable, especially for a team like the Knicks that’s right up against the second apron. The Knicks can’t sign expensive free agents. They have to find value in places other teams overlook.

Now let’s talk about the most interesting angle in this whole story: Jalen Brunson.

Brunson is one of the greatest undersized guards of this era. He was a second-round pick. People said he was too small and not athletic enough. Now he’s a Finals MVP. If there’s anyone in the league who can show a small guard how to survive in the NBA, it’s Brunson. And the things Brunson does well are exactly the things Dillingham needs to learn. Use your body to create space. Get to your spots. Change speeds instead of just going fast. Draw charges and offensive fouls on defense. Understand angles. Make the right read instead of the flashy one.

The video mentions this too, along with players like Marcus Smart and Tyler Kolek, and it’s a fair point. Kolek isn’t the biggest or most athletic guard either. But he’s become a reliable player by making smart decisions and not wasting possessions. Dillingham’s problem isn’t talent. It’s purpose. As the video puts it, he can’t just be quick for no reason. He has to know what he’s doing with that speed. That’s something he can learn in an organization like this.

The video also compares him to his former Kentucky teammate, Bones Hyland, and that’s an interesting comparison. Hyland is another smaller guard who has found a role by bringing pace and hitting big shots off the bench. Dillingham doesn’t have to become a star. He just has to become a player who can come in for fifteen minutes and change the energy of a game. That’s a real role in the NBA, and plenty of players have built long careers doing exactly that.

The video also says the Toronto Raptors considered signing Dillingham before they went with Kevin McCullar Jr. instead. McCullar, of course, was the player the Knicks waived to make room for Knecht. So the G League world is small, and teams are always looking at the same group of young players. That’s another reason Dillingham’s time in Westchester matters. Every scout in the league will be watching.

Why New York, then? The video doesn’t really explain it, but it’s not hard to guess. Westchester is close to the Knicks, the Knicks’ coaches and front office can watch him every week, and the organization has a recent track record of developing guards. For a player trying to get back into the league, that matters. He’ll also get a lot of minutes. Westchester can give him the ball and let him play through mistakes in a way an NBA team defending a title never could. And if he plays well, he’ll be playing well right next door to one of the biggest markets in sports. That’s a smart choice for him, whatever happens next.

Now, let’s be realistic about the downside. The video says it too, and I agree. If Dillingham can’t stand out in the G League, his NBA career could be in real trouble. The G League is full of players who were stars in college and couldn’t make it in the NBA. Being a former lottery pick gives him more chances than most. But at some point, the chances run out. This might be one of his last big opportunities. That’s a lot of pressure for a twenty-one-year-old, but it’s also exactly the kind of challenge that can turn a career around.

So what should we watch for in Westchester? Here’s my checklist. First, efficiency. Dillingham has always been able to score. The question is whether he can score efficiently against bigger, stronger defenders. If he’s shooting well over forty-five percent from the field and around thirty-six percent from three in the G League, that’s a good sign. Second, decision-making. Watch his assist-to-turnover ratio. Small guards who stick in the NBA usually take care of the ball, the way Kolek does. Third, defense. Nobody expects him to become a lockdown defender at one hundred seventy-five pounds. But he has to show effort, smart positioning, and a willingness to take charges. And fourth, consistency. One big game in the G League means nothing. Twenty good games in a row means a lot more.

And here’s how the path to the Knicks would actually work if he plays well. The most likely step is a two-way contract. Two-way players don’t count against the cap or the apron, which is perfect for the Knicks’ situation. They can play up to fifty NBA games. But they aren’t eligible for the playoffs unless they’re converted to a standard contract before the end of the regular season. So if Dillingham ever became good enough to help in the playoffs, the Knicks would need to find a standard roster spot and a minimum salary that fits under the second apron. That’s not easy with this roster. But late in the season, minimum contracts are prorated, so they get cheaper. That’s the kind of small detail that decides whether a move like this ever pays off.

And if you look at this front office’s history, finding value in overlooked players is kind of what it does. Jalen Brunson was a second-round pick. Miles McBride was the thirty-sixth pick. Tyler Kolek was a second-round pick the Knicks traded up to get. Mohamed Diawara was the fifty-first pick. Josh Hart came over in a mid-season trade that a lot of people didn’t think much of at the time. Now all of them are part of a championship team. So when the Knicks take a low-risk swing on a player like Dillingham, it fits a pattern. That’s not genius. It’s just good, patient scouting. And it’s a big reason why this team was able to win a title without a pile of high draft picks.

So is this a genius move by Leon Rose? Let’s tone that down a little. It’s a smart, low-risk move. It costs the Knicks nothing against the cap. If it works, they might have a cheap backup guard for the future. If it doesn’t, they lose nothing. That’s good roster management. The best front offices in the league make a dozen moves like this every season, and most of them never make headlines. But it’s not a masterstroke, and it’s definitely not a massive trade. Front offices make moves like this all the time. Calling it genius sets expectations way too high for a player who’s trying to rebuild his career.

And there’s a bigger picture here that the video does get right near the end. The Knicks are going to face some tough money decisions. Karl-Anthony Towns has a sixty-one million dollar player option and turned down a two-hundred-million-dollar extension offer. Josh Hart has a team option. Mikal Bridges’ salary keeps climbing. Jalen Brunson’s goes up too. ESPN’s Bobby Marks projected the Knicks would be only about fourteen point seven million dollars under the second apron next season if they pick up the options for Towns, Hart and Kolek. When money is that tight, you need cheap players who can actually play. That’s why Kolek matters. That’s why Mohamed Diawara matters. That’s why a Knecht or a Dillingham could matter. It’s not about this week. It’s about the next three years.
