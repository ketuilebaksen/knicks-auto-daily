Here’s the actual news, and it’s the opposite of sad. Hart missed Tuesday’s game against Milwaukee, but according to Mike Brown, it wasn’t because of his ankle. He was sick. Brown said that, based on what he was told, Hart would have played if he hadn’t been feeling under the weather. So the ankle that everybody was worried about isn’t what kept him out. And the Knicks didn’t exactly struggle without him. They beat the Bucks one seventeen to eighty-five. A thirty-two-point win. Four straight wins. Six of their last seven. Six and four on the season. So no, this isn’t a sad story. It’s one of the best weeks the Knicks have had all season. Let’s go through it: Hart’s real status, Tyler Kolek’s big night and the backup point guard battle, Karl-Anthony Towns chasing a Willis Reed record, and the grades for everybody else.

Let’s start with Hart, because that’s what the title is about. Hart has now missed three games this season. The first two, against Chicago and Cleveland, were because of soreness in his right ankle. Brown said at the time that the ankle was nothing major. And on Tuesday, the reason changed. It was an illness, not the ankle. That’s important, because it means the ankle may be fine, or close to it. When a player misses a game because he’s sick, he usually comes back as soon as he’s feeling better. So this looks like good news for Hart’s availability, not bad news.

And the Knicks need him. Through the early part of the season, Hart has averaged about eight point six points, seven point eight rebounds and three point nine assists, according to the video. His shooting has been around thirty-eight and a half percent, which isn’t great. But nobody values Hart for his shooting percentage. He’s the guy who grabs the rebound in traffic, pushes the ball in transition, guards bigger players, and dives on the floor for loose balls. Remember the Brooklyn game? Eight points, six rebounds, seven assists, three steals, and the Knicks were plus twenty-two with him on the floor, the best mark on the team. That’s Josh Hart. The numbers in the box score never tell the whole story.

And having Hart back will help Brown manage minutes. This is a team that played into the middle of June last season. Hart himself said after the Brooklyn game that after a title run, sometimes your body has to catch up. So getting him back healthy, and keeping his minutes reasonable, matters more than winning any one game in November.

Brown also had an interesting message about the oh and three start. He said adversity like that can build character if the players respond the right way, especially when you’re thinking about the games that really matter in April, May and June. And look at how they’ve responded. Since that oh and three start, the Knicks have gone six and one. That’s a team that took the early losses seriously.

Now let’s get to the player who stole the show on Tuesday: Tyler Kolek.

Coming off the bench, in just fifteen minutes, Kolek scored twelve points on five of six shooting and had four assists. That’s about as efficient as it gets. And it’s not a one-night thing. When Jose Alvarado was out with his ankle sprain earlier this season, Kolek had twenty-five assists and only two turnovers in fifty-seven minutes, according to SNY. Even after his rough night in Cleveland, where he had a season-high number of turnovers, Sports Illustrated noted his assist-to-turnover ratio for the season was still thirty-six to four. And on Tuesday, he bounced right back.

The part that has fans talking is Alvarado’s minutes. Alvarado only played six minutes against Milwaukee. Miles McBride also played more than him, but McBride is more of a combo guard who can play on the wing. Kolek is a natural point guard. So the question everyone is asking is simple: has Kolek taken the backup point guard job?

Brown was careful with his answer. He said Kolek is playing excellent basketball right now, but he made it clear that doesn’t mean there’s a problem with Alvarado. He said everyone has to stay ready, because their number can be called at any time. And here’s the key: he didn’t promise Alvarado would get his minutes back, and he didn’t officially name Kolek the permanent backup either. The competition is still open.

And that’s exactly what Brown told both players a couple of weeks ago. Remember, before the Brooklyn game, he sat them both down. He told Alvarado, you’re our backup because you ended the season as the backup. He reminded them that both had helped win last year, Alvarado in the Finals and Kolek in the Cup final. And then he said, both of you have to be ready, because a change can happen at any time. Change for a half, or change for five games, who knows. Well, the change may be happening. And Brown is doing exactly what he said he would do.

For Kolek, this could be a big moment. Last season, he got real minutes early, but his role shrank as the year went on, and in the playoffs, most of his minutes came in blowouts. This year, he’s playing with more confidence. He’s making quick decisions, he’s scoring when the defense gives him space, and he’s running the second unit the way Brown wants: singles, not home runs. After the loss in Philadelphia, Brown said the team couldn’t try to hit home runs on offense. It had to hit singles. Kolek is a singles hitter. That’s why he fits. He doesn’t force anything, and he doesn’t waste possessions.

And let’s remember who Kolek is, because his story makes this even better. He was the Big East Player of the Year at Marquette in twenty twenty-three. The Knicks traded up into the second round to get him in twenty twenty-four. Last season, he played sixty-two games and shot around thirty-nine percent from three. And in the NBA Cup final, he had fourteen points, five rebounds and five assists. That’s the night Brown was talking about when he said Tyler helped win the Cup. He’s also on one of the cheapest contracts on the roster, about two point three million dollars this season, with a team option for next season at about two point five million. Back in October, Marc Stein reported that the Knicks weren’t shopping Kolek, and that they’d only move him for a coveted center. So the front office has believed in him for a while. Now he’s showing why.

But don’t count Alvarado out. He’s the defensive spark. He’s the guy with the Grand Theft Alvarado steals. He played real minutes in the Finals. And Brown has shown he’ll change his rotation depending on the opponent. Against a team with a quick, aggressive point guard, Alvarado’s defense might be exactly what Brown wants. So this isn’t a demotion. It’s a competition. And competitions are healthy for a team trying to repeat.

Alvarado’s story is a good one too, and it’s worth remembering why Brown is being so careful with him. He went undrafted out of Georgia Tech. He started on a two-way deal in New Orleans and built a whole reputation on sneaky inbound steals. The Knicks got him at the deadline last February, when McBride needed core muscle surgery, and he ended up playing real minutes in the Finals. Players like that don’t need guarantees. They need a chance. Brown made it clear Alvarado lost his minutes because of the ankle sprain, not because he did anything wrong. Now he has to win them back, and he will get chances.

And here’s a roster angle that matters for the trade deadline. On December fifteenth, Alvarado becomes trade-eligible, along with Jordan Clarkson, Mohamed Diawara and Andre Drummond. The Knicks have a lot of guards: Brunson, McBride, Alvarado, Kolek, Clarkson and Shamet, plus Hart, who plays like a guard on a lot of possessions. That’s too many for one rotation. And the Knicks’ biggest need is still a backup center who can protect the rim. So if Kolek really does take over the backup point guard job, it gives the Knicks more freedom to use one or two of those guards in a trade for size. Nobody has reported anything like that. But that’s how roster depth turns into trade flexibility. Kolek’s emergence doesn’t just help on the court. It could help the front office too.

Now let’s talk about Karl-Anthony Towns, because he’s chasing some real history. Towns had another double-double on Tuesday, and according to the video, he’s now one away from tying Willis Reed’s franchise record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season. Willis Reed. The captain of the Knicks’ first two championship teams. That’s the kind of company Towns is in right now. He got an A from Sports Illustrated’s grades, with strong defense, dominance on the boards and a physical presence the Bucks couldn’t handle.

If you don’t know the name, Willis Reed is one of the most important players in Knicks history. He was the captain of the Knicks’ championship teams in nineteen seventy and nineteen seventy-three. He was the Finals MVP both times. And he gave us one of the most famous moments in NBA history, when he limped out of the tunnel for Game Seven of the nineteen seventy Finals and inspired the Garden. For Towns to be mentioned in the same sentence as Reed, chasing his record, in a season after the Knicks won their first title since nineteen seventy-three, is pretty special. Every double-double from here gets him closer to a piece of franchise history.

And look at how he got here. Nineteen and twelve in the opener against Philadelphia. Twenty-two and fourteen in Orlando. Twenty-seven and twenty against Detroit. Twenty-two and twelve in the Cup game in Philadelphia. Eleven and sixteen against Brooklyn. Seventeen and twelve against Chicago. Twenty-nine and thirteen in Cleveland. And now another one against Milwaukee. He’s averaging close to fourteen rebounds a game. That’s not a hot streak. That’s consistency at an elite level. Big lineups, small lineups, good defenses, bad defenses, at home and on the road. Every single night, he’s given the Knicks a double-double. That’s the kind of reliability you can build a title defense around.

Think about the timing of that. Over the past week, we’ve seen a lot of videos about Towns’ contract, the two hundred million dollar offer, the player option, and whether the Knicks might trade him. Meanwhile, Towns has just kept doing his job. Twenty-nine and thirteen in Cleveland. Another double-double against Milwaukee. And now he’s chasing a franchise record that belongs to one of the greatest Knicks ever. That’s the best answer anyone could give to the contract noise.

Jalen Brunson also got an A. He didn’t need huge numbers. When Milwaukee tried to make a run in the third quarter, Brunson made the right plays at the right times and shut it down. Coming off back-to-back thirty-point games against Chicago and Cleveland, this was a different kind of game for him. Control, not volume. That’s what great point guards do in blowouts. They make sure the blowout stays a blowout.

Now the rest of the grades.

Andre Drummond owned the paint. Fourteen rebounds in just sixteen minutes. That’s almost a rebound a minute. After Brown named him the defensive player of the game in Cleveland, Drummond followed it up with another huge night on the glass. The backup center spot has been the biggest question on this roster all season. Drummond is giving the Knicks a real answer, at least on the boards.

Mikal Bridges earned a B-plus. In seventeen minutes, he had five rebounds and three steals and brought a lot of defensive energy. That’s important, because Bridges has been at the center of a lot of trade talk this month. His three-point shooting is still a problem, around twenty-seven percent for the season. But in Cleveland, he had six rebounds, two blocks and four assists. And against Milwaukee, five rebounds and three steals. He’s finding ways to help when the shot isn’t falling. That’s exactly what the Knicks need from him.

OG Anunoby was another standout. He hit five threes and added eight points in transition. After a rough defensive night in Cleveland, where Donovan Mitchell got going in the fourth quarter, this was a strong bounce-back.

Landry Shamet earned a B-plus, starting again in Hart’s place and shooting efficiently from deep. He’s been one of the Knicks’ best shooters all season.

Miles McBride had eight points and a plus-minus of plus thirty-nine. Plus thirty-nine. That’s a crazy number, even in a blowout. It tells you the Knicks absolutely dominated the minutes he was on the floor.

Jordan Clarkson had three offensive rebounds in eleven minutes. Mohamed Diawara had a quiet but positive game.

And then there’s Pacôme Dadiet, who earned an A after a strong fourth quarter. He converted a three-point play, grabbed an offensive rebound, and hit a three. That’s a great sign for a young player who hasn’t gotten many minutes this season. Remember, the Knicks declined his option for next season at the end of October, so he’s on an expiring contract. Nights like this help him, whether that’s earning more minutes in New York or showing other teams what he can do.

James Wiseman, Dalton Knecht and Jose Alvarado didn’t play enough for a real grade.

And when Hart returns, Brown has a fun puzzle. Does Hart go straight back into the starting lineup, with Shamet returning to the bench? That’s the most likely answer. Hart has been a starter on this team for a long time, and his rebounding and defense are a big part of the starting group’s identity. But Shamet has played well in the starting spot, giving Brunson and Towns more spacing. Brown could also ease Hart back with a minutes limit, especially after an illness. Either way, it’s a good problem to have. A month ago, people were asking how the Knicks would survive without Hart. Now they’re asking how to fit him back in.

So let’s step back. What does this win tell us?

First, the Knicks are rolling. Four straight wins. Six of seven. Six and four. A thirty-two-point win over Milwaukee, after a thirty-point win over Chicago and a road win in Cleveland. That’s three convincing wins in a week. And the scary part for the rest of the league is that they did all of it without Josh Hart, and without Mikal Bridges playing at his best. When both of those players are back to normal, this team gets a lot more dangerous. That’s the version of the Knicks that won the title last June, and it might not be far away.

Second, the depth is real. The Knicks won three straight games without Josh Hart. Kolek, McBride, Shamet, Drummond, Dadiet, everybody is contributing. The bench isn’t just surviving. It’s winning minutes.

Third, the Hart news is good news. It was illness on Tuesday, not the ankle. That means the ankle may not be the long-term worry some people feared. When he’s back, the Knicks get their toughest player back, and Brown gets more options.

And fourth, Brown is managing this team the way he said he would. He told the players that minutes are earned, and that change can happen at any time. Kolek earned more minutes. Shamet earned a starting spot while Hart was out. Drummond earned the defensive player of the game. Dadiet earned fourth-quarter minutes and made the most of them. That’s how you keep a deep team sharp.

And there’s a lesson in how the Knicks have handled the soft part of the schedule. Good teams don’t just beat bad teams. They beat them early and often, so the starters can rest and the young players can get real minutes. Against Chicago, the Knicks led by forty-four. Against Milwaukee, they won by thirty-two. In both games, Brunson didn’t have to carry a heavy load in the fourth quarter. That’s how you save legs for a long season. And it’s how players like Dadiet and Knecht get the reps they need to be ready when Brown calls their number in a big game.

Now, let’s be fair about Milwaukee. The Bucks were widely expected to struggle this season, and they’ve been one of the teams near the bottom of the standings. So this win, like the Chicago win, came against a team that isn’t at the Knicks’ level. But you can only beat who’s in front of you, and the Knicks have been doing that in a big way.

Next up is another game against Cleveland, the team the Knicks just beat on the road and swept in last season’s conference finals. That’s the real test. If the Knicks can beat a healthier Cavaliers team again, this stretch starts to look like the team that won it all last June.

And one last word on the title. Josh Hart sad news? No. Josh Hart was sick, he would have played otherwise, and his team won by thirty-two without him. The real news is good news. A more honest title would be something like, it wasn’t the ankle: Josh Hart update, and Tyler Kolek’s big night. Or, Knicks crush Bucks by thirty-two as Kolek makes his case.
