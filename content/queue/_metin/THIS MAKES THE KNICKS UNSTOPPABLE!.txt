The Knicks' fourteenth roster spot was supposed to be the hardest decision of camp. In the end, an injury made it for them. Drew Eubanks tore a pectoral muscle, the Knicks waived him along with Tony Bradley and Ochai Agbaji, and James Wiseman is now the clear favorite to be the third center on the defending champions.

But here's the thing most coverage is skipping. The fourteenth spot isn't the question anymore. The question now is the fifteenth. The Knicks clearly want to keep Bruce Brown too, and there's only one way that happens. So today, we'll do this in order: what's actually confirmed, why Eubanks's injury matters more than people realize, Wiseman's real numbers, and then the trade the Knicks are reportedly exploring to keep both players, with the arithmetic that decides whether it works.

And one quick note on the word "unstoppable" in the title I've seen on these videos all week. A third center doesn't make anybody unstoppable. What he can do is keep a good team from falling apart when something goes wrong. That's less exciting, but it's what actually wins in April.

Welcome back to Ketuil KNICKS. Let's go.

First, what's confirmed.

On Thursday, Shams Charania reported that Drew Eubanks had suffered a torn pectoral muscle and would be waived. Shortly after, Shams added that the Knicks were also waiving Tony Bradley and Ochai Agbaji, and the Knicks' PR account made all three official. Eubanks is expected to miss an extended period, and some reports say the season. Bradley had been dealing with a hamstring problem since the Toronto game on Tuesday. Agbaji had struggled, including an oh-for-six night from three against Minnesota.

So the camp field went from five to two. John Konchar left earlier for personal reasons. Now Eubanks, Bradley and Agbaji are gone. The two players still standing on Exhibit 9 deals are James Wiseman and Bruce Brown. And on Thursday, Ian Begley of SNY reported that Wiseman has emerged as the favorite for the fourteenth spot, and that Bruce Brown has impressed in camp.

Here's what isn't confirmed yet. As of right now, Wiseman hasn't signed a standard contract. The roster isn't final. And nothing has happened with Bruce Brown, either way. The Knicks have until just before Tuesday's opener against Philadelphia to set the roster.

Now let's talk about Eubanks for a minute, because he deserves more than a line in a roster update.

Drew Eubanks is twenty-nine. He went undrafted out of Oregon State in 2018 and worked his way into the league on a two-way deal in San Antonio. Since then he's played more than four hundred NBA games for the Spurs, Portland, Phoenix, Utah, the Clippers and Sacramento. He was never a star. He was the kind of professional who shows up, does the dirty work and gets another contract. His last season in Sacramento ended in March with surgery on a torn ligament in his left thumb. He came to New York on a tryout deal to rebuild his value, and he tore a pectoral muscle in camp.

That's a brutal sequence. And it's the reason the Exhibit 9 contract exists. That type of camp deal limits what a team owes a player who gets hurt during camp. It means the Knicks aren't on the hook for a full season of salary, and it's part of why every one of these camp players was signed on one. It's the business side, and it's cold. I hope Eubanks recovers fully and gets another shot somewhere. He's earned it.

And here's why his injury matters for the Knicks specifically. It removes the safe option. Remember Ian Begley's argument a couple of weeks ago. If the third center is just a bridge until the trade deadline, you want the most reliable bridge, and that was Eubanks: four hundred games, playoff experience, no surprises. That argument made sense. With Eubanks gone, the Knicks don't have a low-risk veteran center left in camp. They have Wiseman, the high-upside, higher-risk option, and that's it. The decision got made, but the margin for error got smaller.

Now, Wiseman, with real numbers, because there's a lot of exaggeration going around in both directions.

He's twenty-five. Six-eleven, about two hundred forty pounds. Second overall pick in 2020. The last time he had a full season was 2023-24 in Detroit, and it was better than his reputation. Sixty-three games, about seventeen minutes a night, seven point one points, five point three rebounds, and sixty-one percent from the field. That's a useful backup-center line. The weakness in that season was defense. He averaged only about half a block a game, which is low for someone with his size and athleticism.

Then came the injury. He signed with Indiana and tore his Achilles in the first game of the 2024-25 season. Since then he's played only about five NBA games across two and a half years, including a brief stint with the Pacers and a ten-day contract last December. And this preseason with the Knicks has been his comeback. Eight points and five rebounds in about nine minutes in Philadelphia. Playing time against Washington when the other centers didn't. And then the eighteen points and ten rebounds on seven of eight shooting in Toronto, in a game the Knicks lost by fifty with the regulars resting.

So what's the honest scouting report? On offense, he's a rim runner. Most of his shots come at the basket. He'll take an open midrange shot, but he doesn't shoot threes and doesn't need the ball. He sets screens, rolls hard, catches lobs and finishes. He can make a simple pass out of the high post. That's exactly the role the Knicks need. Drummond is a bruiser who rolls, but he doesn't play above the rim the way Wiseman does. Towns pops out to the three-point line. Wiseman gives Brunson a vertical target in the pick-and-roll, and that's something the Knicks haven't had since Mitchell Robinson left for Boston.

On defense, there are still real questions. In Toronto, his drop coverage was too deep, giving up pull-up shots and drives into his body. He leaned into shooters at the rim instead of going straight up, which led to fouls. And he had no blocks in about twenty-four minutes. Can he be the defender Mitchell Robinson was? No. Not this season, and maybe never. Robinson was one of the best offensive rebounders and rim deterrents in the league. But Wiseman doesn't have to be Robinson. He has to be good enough for six to ten minutes on the nights when Towns or Drummond can't go. And he moves well laterally for his size, which gives the coaching staff something to work with.

One more thing about fit. Last season, the Knicks didn't trust their young backup bigs in big moments. Ariel Hukporti, who's now in Philadelphia, wasn't someone Brown leaned on in the biggest moments. When the Knicks needed size against a big lineup, Mike Brown often went small, with OG Anunoby guarding bigger players. A third center who can hold up for a few minutes against size gives Brown another option, so he doesn't have to stretch Anunoby every time.

So yes, I think the Knicks are making the right call with Wiseman. Not because he's guaranteed to work. Because his contract won't be guaranteed until the league-wide date in early January, the cost of being wrong is small, and the upside is a real rotation big at a minimum salary. Bobby Marks called him a flyer. That's the right word.

Now, the bigger question. The fifteenth spot, and Bruce Brown.

You know the basic math by now. The Knicks have thirteen guaranteed contracts. They're about three point two seven million dollars under the second apron, and ownership treats that line as a hard ceiling. A one-year veteran minimum counts about two point four five million against the apron. So one fits and two don't. If Wiseman takes the fourteenth spot, there's no room for Bruce Brown. Unless the Knicks move salary.

And they're trying. Brett Siegel of ClutchPoints reported that the Knicks are exploring trade options for Pacôme Dadiet. James L. Edwards the third of The Athletic said on SNY that he thinks the Knicks will find a way to keep Bruce Brown, in his words, "whether now or later." And a Knicks On SI piece mentioned Tyler Kolek as another possible piece, as the third point guard on a roster with Brunson, McBride and Alvarado, though there's no reporting that the Knicks are shopping him.

So let's do the arithmetic, because this is the whole story.

Dadiet makes just under three million dollars this season, about two point nine eight million. If the Knicks trade him to a team that can take in his salary without sending any salary back, either because it has cap room or a trade exception, the Knicks' payroll drops by about three million. Their cushion under the second apron goes from about three point two seven million to about six point two five million. Two veteran minimums cost about four point nine million. Six point two five minus four point nine leaves about one point three million to spare. So Wiseman and Bruce Brown would both fit, and the Knicks would still be under the second apron.

But the roster count matters too. Trade Dadiet, and the Knicks drop from thirteen guaranteed players to twelve. Add Wiseman and Bruce Brown, and you're at fourteen. Not fifteen. So that's the real outcome of a Dadiet trade: you swap a young wing for a veteran wing, keep your third center, and stay at fourteen. It doesn't open a fifteenth spot. It changes who's in the fourteenth.

That's an important detail. People keep saying "trade Dadiet to keep both." Keeping both is right. But the roster would still have no fifteenth man and no room for anybody else. Every injury this season would still test the bottom of the roster, and the two-way players, Kevin McCullar, Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins, would still be the next men up.

Now, who would take Dadiet? This is where it gets hard. He's a 2024 first-round pick, twenty-one years old, a long, athletic wing who's still raw. In the preseason he averaged about seven points in fifteen minutes, shooting about thirty-five percent from the field and thirty-three from three. He had ten points in the finale against Toronto, but he went one for seven from three. Edwards reported earlier this week that there hasn't been much interest. For a team to take a young player into its cap room, it usually wants something for its trouble, typically a second-round pick, or at least the belief that the player can become something. The Knicks' remaining draft capital is thin, so they might have to attach a second-round pick to move a first-round pick. That's the price of the apron.

And there's a timing detail that makes this more interesting. Dadiet is on his rookie-scale contract, and the deadline for teams to decide on rookie-scale options for next season is October 31st. The Knicks have to decide by then whether to pick up his fourth-year option for 2027-28. If they're already shopping him, you can probably guess which way they're leaning. But a trade before then makes the decision someone else's problem.

What about Tyler Kolek instead? It's been mentioned, so let me be fair to the idea. Kolek is the third point guard behind Brunson, and on most nights McBride and Alvarado will be ahead of him too. So on paper, he's the most duplicated skill set on the roster. But I'd be surprised. Kolek had one of the best nights of any reserve in the opener in Philadelphia, he's the kind of organizer Mike Brown has praised, and the Knicks paused extension talks with him rather than ending them. You don't usually pause talks with a player you're trying to move. Dadiet is the more natural candidate because he's further from the rotation and because of the option decision coming up.

And let me explain why the two-way players don't solve this either, because people ask about it in the comments. The Knicks' three two-way slots are filled by Kevin McCullar, Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins. Two-way players don't count against the cap or the apron, which is why they're so valuable for a team like this. But they're limited to fifty games on the active roster during the regular season, and they can't play in the playoffs unless they're converted to a standard contract. And converting one to a standard contract would count against the apron exactly like any other minimum signing. So a two-way player isn't a hidden fifteenth man. He's a part-time depth piece for the regular season. And notice that none of the three is a center. That's another reason the Knicks needed Wiseman on a standard deal rather than hoping a two-way big would cover for injuries.

As for the players who were cut, all three go on waivers for forty-eight hours, and any team can claim their contracts. Claims on non-guaranteed camp deals are rare, so the usual outcome is that they clear and become free agents. Agbaji is a former lottery pick, fourteenth overall in 2022, and he's only twenty-six, so I'd expect someone to give him another chance at some point this season. Bradley, if the hamstring is minor, is the kind of big man teams call in December when injuries pile up. And Eubanks will be focused on rehab first.

So should the Knicks do it? Let me give you both sides.

The case for trading Dadiet and keeping Bruce Brown is that this is a win-now roster. Brown is a thirty-year-old champion who played all eighty-two games last season, defends multiple positions and plays with the edge Mike Brown has been asking for. Brunson said Brown brings a lot to the table. And Dadiet, frankly, isn't in the rotation on a title team. He's a development project, and the Knicks' timeline is now.

The case against is that Dadiet is twenty-one, a first-round pick, and the Knicks have very few young assets left. If he becomes a rotation player in two years, you'd regret giving him away, possibly with a pick attached, for a one-year minimum veteran. And Bruce Brown, for all his strengths, averaged about three point seven points in three preseason games. He didn't force the decision on the court the way Wiseman did.

My view? I'd make the trade if the price is just a second-round pick. A title defense is about the next eight months, and the Knicks' bench defense has been the biggest weakness all preseason. Thirty-nine fast-break points in Philadelphia. Eighteen threes from Minnesota. Thirty fast-break points in one half in Toronto. Bruce Brown is a direct answer to that problem. Dadiet, at twenty-one, isn't yet. But if the price is more than a second, or if no one will take Dadiet without a bigger sweetener, I'd keep him, sign Wiseman, and wait. As Edwards said, "whether now or later." Brown could become available again in-season if he goes unsigned, and the Knicks' flexibility opens up around December 15th, when this summer's signings become tradable.

Now, how does the rotation look heading into Tuesday?

The starters are set. Brunson, Bridges, Anunoby, Towns and Hart. Mike Brown named them himself and said everything behind them is open. From the preseason, the likely bench order looks something like this. Landry Shamet as the first guard off the bench, maybe the sixth man after his preseason shooting. Deuce McBride and Jose Alvarado in the guard rotation. Mohamed Diawara on the wing. Andre Drummond as the backup center. Then Jordan Clarkson and Tyler Kolek as situational options. And then Wiseman as the third center, plus Bruce Brown if he's kept.

That's a deep group, and it's why the Knicks are reluctant to spend assets on a fifteenth man. But depth on paper and depth in a game are different things. The preseason showed that the second unit's defense has to get a lot better before it can hold leads against good teams.

And one more note for anyone wondering about other options. Trey Jemison, who spent last season with the Knicks on a two-way deal, was waived by Toronto this week. He's a six-ten center who knows the system. There's no reporting that the Knicks are interested, and a two-way reunion would mean cutting one of McCullar, Nickel or Akins. But he's a name worth knowing if Wiseman's body doesn't hold up.

So what should you watch for in the next four days?

One, Wiseman's contract. When it's announced, look at whether it's guaranteed. A non-guaranteed deal is the Knicks protecting themselves on the health question. A guaranteed deal would be a big vote of confidence.

Two, a Dadiet trade. If he's moved before Tuesday, check what the Knicks give up. If it's only a second-round pick, it's a win-now move that makes sense. If it's more, the price of keeping Bruce Brown just went up.

Three, Bruce Brown's status. If the Knicks waive him without a trade, watch whether he signs elsewhere quickly. If he goes unsigned, Edwards's "whether now or later" might come true.

And four, Tuesday night. Opening night at the Garden, banner night, against LeBron and the Sixers. If Wiseman checks in during the first half, that tells you how much Mike Brown already trusts him.
